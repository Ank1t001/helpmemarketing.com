Why the number one account published no video

Pier 4 moves to first with 72 on 15 posts and no reel in the window. What we observed: its Distribution pillar compares peak plays with followers, and with no video in the window there is no reach to measure, so under the published method it sits at the honest end of that pillar. Its Intent score comes from 5 comments on 85 likes, a ratio of 5.88 percent. What it may suggest: the lead over McGillivray Capital rests on how an absence of video is scored as much as on what Pier 4 posted. What to test: whether the order holds next cycle if Pier 4 publishes a reel.

The scoring method has not changed since cycle 003, and the same treatment applied to Lankin when it published no video in cycle 003. We are publishing the sensitivity rather than adjusting the method mid-series.

Read this before you read the rest of the ranks

54 comments across 57 posts in thirty days, and Intent is 30 percent of the score. 27 belong to Lankin, 21 to McGillivray Capital (18 of them on one reel), 5 to Pier 4, 1 to Equiton. Skyline recorded none while posting the second highest engagement rate on the board.

Lankin’s comments arrived at 1 to 4 per post on posts with a median of 3.5 likes, a comment to like ratio of 47.4 percent. Above 5 percent the Intent band decays, so that volume lifts Lankin less than it seems. Public data does not show who the commenters are, so we report the pattern without characterising it. Treat the ranks as a reading of a thin signal.

Your takeaway, any category

The category story

The category story

A paid category with a quiet feed.

The narrative finding of cycle 005

Cycles 001 to 003 read this as a category where native accounts do better than cross-posted ones and the smallest accounts are the ones being answered. This cycle adds a fact the feed alone could not show: all five firms are paying for Meta distribution, at least 72 live investor-facing ads between them, and none of that spend is visible in the organic numbers this index scores.

The paid feed and the organic feed are saying different things. Skyline Wealth Management started 16 investor ads between 6 and 9 October while Skyline’s own feed posted leasing milestones and a charity golf day. Pier 4 has run investor ads since June, many placed on Instagram only, while its feed published no reel in this window.

So the suggestion is narrow. In this sample, posts about people and community drew the most likes per post and market commentary the least. The exceptions on the same board are named in the theme table above, and one cycle is not a pattern. We will hold it against cycle 006.

Your takeaway, any category

Read a competitor’s feed and its ad library together. The feed shows what an audience chose to engage with; the ad library shows what the firm chose to pay for. Either alone misleads.

High posting, low visible engagement

High posting, low visible engagement

The largest audience on the board and the lowest engagement rate this cycle.

Equiton, 18,409 followers, 14 posts, 11.5 median likes

Equiton published 14 times in thirty days, 8 of them reels, to the largest audience in the index, and drew 1 comment. Its engagement rate of 0.062 percent is the lowest on the board. Its 5 market round ups drew a median of 9 likes.

At the same time it had at least 25 ads live in the Meta ad library, some running since 1 April, including an investor evening that began advertising on 6 October. What we observed: heavy paid activity alongside the quietest organic feed per follower. What it may suggest: the organic feed is not where this firm is trying to win attention. What to test: whether organic posts that echo a live ad’s message draw more than the round ups do.

Its best organic post this cycle was a lifestyle reel about not having to deal with a broken dishwasher, 25 likes, about twice its median.

Your takeaway, any category

Posting volume and ad spend are inputs. Engagement per follower is the output. When inputs rise and the output does not move, the content is the place to look first.

What this ledger can and cannot see

Four of five flags read ORGANIC and all five firms had live investor ads. A clean flag is not a clean category. The flag measures organic reach on each firm’s own handle. It cannot see ads that never appear in the feed, spend placed from separate pages, budget, objective or targeting, and play counts include replays and autoplays. The one BOOST flag this cycle comes from a co-authored post, which the instrument also cannot tell apart from paid reach. The right output from any row is a question for that firm’s marketing lead, not a verdict.

How the ad counts were taken

The library shows that an ad is live; it does not show budget, reach or results.

Frequently asked

Did the roster change this cycle?

No. The five firms are the same as cycle 003: Skyline, Equiton, Lankin, Pier 4 and McGillivray Capital. Marlin Spring left at cycle 003 after three consecutive windows at two posts. Because Resonance and Distribution are percentile pillars, any future roster change re-ranks every firm, and the page will say so.

Why do flags read ORGANIC when every firm is running ads?

Because the flag measures one thing: peak plays on the firm’s own handle against its followers. Ads placed from a separate page, or ads that never appear in the feed, do not add to those plays. The ad library count is published beneath each flag so the two facts sit side by side. A clean flag is not a clean category.

Why is Pier 4 first with no video in the window?

Distribution compares peak plays with followers. With no reel there is nothing pushing that ratio up, and under the method used since cycle 003 that places Pier 4 at the honest end of the pillar. The same treatment applied to Lankin in cycle 003. If Pier 4’s Distribution were scored at the middle of the pool instead, it would fall behind McGillivray Capital. We publish that sensitivity rather than change the method mid series.

What does BOOST 67x mean for McGillivray Capital?

Its best reel was co-authored with @scott_mcgillivray, which has 351,567 followers. Co-authored posts are shown to both audiences, so plays measured against the firm’s 646 followers look like a media buy even if the reach came from the founder’s audience. Against both audiences combined the reel ran 0.12x. The flag is arithmetically correct; the explanation it implies may not be.

Why is Wealthsimple on the page if it is not a real estate firm?

As a reference only. It is never pooled into any median, percentile or score, it does not appear on the scoreboard, and its rows in the downloadable CSV are tagged REFERENCE. It drew 200 comments on 12 posts in the same window that the five ranked firms drew 54 on 57.

Why like per play rather than like per view?

Instagram’s view counts are inconsistently reported and sometimes lower than like counts, which produces impossible ratios. Plays are reported reliably, so every resonance number on this page uses plays as the denominator.

How does the vision pass work, and what are its limits?

It reads the opening frame of each firm’s top post of the window, so the creative findings are independent of the captions and the metrics. All five frames were read this cycle. For a reel, the frame is the cover image, not the whole video. Where a frame cannot be read, this page labels it unreviewed and does not describe it.

Is any of the data estimated?

No. Every Instagram number comes from public data collected on 13 October 2026, and every claim links to its source post. Ad counts are minimums from the Meta ad library on the same day, grouped by the advertiser page that placed them.
