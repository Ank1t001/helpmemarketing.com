The number that should stop you

Skyline gets 0.878 percent engagement on 2,222 followers. Equiton gets 0.072 percent on 17,974. Twelve times the resonance on roughly one eighth of the audience. The gap narrowed from sixteen times, and it narrowed because Skyline came down, not because Equiton climbed.

The two big moves are honest but fragile. Lankin climbs three places on a comment to like ratio built from exactly three comments across thirteen posts. Pier 4 falls one place and 26 points because it recorded zero comments on 113 likes across fifteen posts, which zeroes the Intent pillar outright. Both readings are correct and both would move again on a handful of comments. Treat the ranks as provisional until cycle 004.

What the top of the board is actually doing

Read from all 67 captions and the creative itself. Language and artifacts, not just counting.

Skyline, number one

Still native, quietly cooling

The only firm treating Instagram as its own channel rather than a mirror, and it holds first place for a second cycle. But the mix moved hard toward stills, 9 statics against 2 reels, and the engagement rate slipped from 0.996 to 0.878 percent. 20 median likes on 2,222 followers, and the highest like per play in the index at 2.6 percent. The audience is small and entirely real. The camera is coming out less often.

Equiton, number two

The week it had something to say

The heaviest publisher again at 25 posts, still mostly explainer carousels and weekly market round ups, still a median of zero comments. Then it launched a US fund and one post pulled 112 likes, nine times its own median. The lesson is not that Equiton should post more. It is that thirty market round ups moved nothing and one real announcement moved everything.

Lankin, number three

The escape, on three comments

Last cycle's cautionary tale climbs to third. The cross posting has not stopped and the median is still 2 likes on 3,826 followers, but two things changed: a softball team photo pulled 28 likes, their best of the window, and three posts drew comments where cycle 001 drew none. That is enough to move a pillar and it is not enough to call a recovery.

Pier 4, number four

Good creative, silent room

Their intern reels are the two best posts on the account and the secondary market thesis is now a real editorial line. None of it drew a single comment. Zero comments across fifteen posts collapses the Intent pillar and costs them 26 points. The creative is not the problem here, the absence of any conversation is.

Your takeaway, any category

Captions and creative are strategy documents your competitors publish for free. Two cycles of them tells you who is native, who is mirroring, and which movements in a ranking are real signal rather than a small sample moving around.

The pattern across eight frames

Five Instagram frames re-harvested this cycle, and they refine last cycle's rule rather than repeat it. The frames that win still open on people the audience might actually know: a staff party, an intern mid sentence. The frame that loses hardest still opens on empty staged space, a model suite with the table set and nobody home, on the largest idle audience in the index. But Equiton broke the pattern with a human free architectural render, and it broke it because the post carried a real announcement. Craft loses to people. People lose to news.

The creative positioning map

Every axis is a measured number. X, paid push: peak plays divided by followers, log scale, how hard the reach was pushed. Y, resonance: median like per play, whether people who saw it responded. Instagram's view counts are unreliable, so plays are the denominator throughout. Hover any circle.

Where the empty space is, the actionable bit

Top right is empty for a second cycle: nobody in Canadian private real estate pushes big and lands. Pier 4 pushed 183x in June and resonance on that reel collapsed to 0.02 percent. Skyline lands at 2.6 percent and pushes nothing. The first firm to put media money behind Skyline-grade native creative takes a lane with no competition in it, and it has now been vacant for two consecutive cycles.

Your takeaway, any category

Plot your market on these two axes and the strategy writes itself: never out-spend the bought and bouncing corner. Out-resonate it.

The category story

Native or nothing, confirmed.

The narrative finding of cycle 002

Cycle 001 said this category is one where the institutions are absent, the mirrors are dying and the natives are winning. Cycle 002 tests that and it holds. Three of eight firms still have no account. Skyline, the only firm producing for the platform, holds first place. The bought spike from June is still the largest reach event in the index and still has left nothing behind: Pier 4's median is 5 likes.

What cycle 002 adds is a second lever. Equiton's single best post in two cycles was not better creative, it was real news, a US fund launch that pulled nine times its median from a frame with no human being in it. Against thirty weekly market round ups that moved nothing, that is the clearest instruction on the page.

On this platform, in this category, distribution cannot be bought and cannot be mirrored. It has to be made, or it has to be earned with something worth announcing.

Your takeaway, any category

One brand changing message is noise. The same pattern holding across two consecutive cycles is the market telling you what the channel requires. Watch the pattern, not the post.

The cautionary tale

The largest idle audience in the index.

Marlin Spring Developments, 14,942 followers, 2 posts in 30 days

Two posts in this window. Two posts in the last one. The second largest audience on the page and the smallest output on it, for the second cycle running. Their best post of the window is a beautifully produced, completely empty model suite: table set for four, skyline through the window, nobody home. It earned 19 likes from 14,942 followers.

Cycle 001 put Lankin in this slot for posting into silence. Lankin has since climbed to third, not by fixing its content but by drawing a handful of comments and one genuinely human post. Marlin Spring has not moved at all, which is why the slot changes hands.

This is not a content quality problem. It is an abandonment problem: roughly 14,900 people opted in and are being sent two posts a month of buyer-facing offer language.

Your takeaway, any category

An audience you built and stopped talking to is a liability on the balance sheet, not an asset. If you cannot resource the channel, say so and close it. Leaving it half alive is the worst of the three options.

Frequently asked

Why only five accounts?

Because only five of the eight firms tracked in the LinkedIn index have an Instagram account. Starlight, Centurion and Hazelview have none, verified against their own websites and by direct search, and re-verified at cycle 002. The index reports what exists rather than padding the table.

How is this scored differently from the LinkedIn index?

Instagram publishes plays, so Resonance can use like per play and Distribution honesty can use peak plays divided by followers, where lower is better. LinkedIn publishes neither, so its index substitutes share to like. They are deliberately different instruments and the scores are not comparable across the two pages.

Why like per play rather than like per view?

Instagram's view counts are inconsistently reported and sometimes lower than like counts, which produces impossible ratios. Plays are reported reliably, so every resonance number on this page uses plays as the denominator and says so.

Why did two accounts move so far in one cycle?

Because the Intent pillar is 30 percent of the score and it is built on comments, which are extremely scarce in this category.
