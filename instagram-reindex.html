Coverage, five of eight tracked firms

Starlight, Centurion and Hazelview have no Instagram account. Not dormant, none exists. Their websites link LinkedIn and nothing else, and the handles that surface in search belong to other companies.

That is itself the first finding of this index. The three largest institutional firms in Canadian private real estate have collectively decided Instagram is not their channel. Read every number below against that.

The number that should stop you

Skyline gets 0.996 percent engagement on 2,209 followers. Equiton gets 0.062 percent on 17,855. Sixteen times the resonance on one eighth of the audience.

Lankin is starker still: 3,681 followers and a median of 1 like. Four of their ten posts in the window scored zero.

What the top of the board is actually doing

Read from all 59 captions and the creative itself. Language and artifacts, not just counting.

Skyline, number one

The native channel

The only firm treating Instagram as its own channel rather than a mirror: construction reels, event title cards, a consistent visual system. 22 median likes on 2,209 followers, and the highest like per play in the index at 3.3 percent. The audience is small and entirely real.

Equiton, number two

Cadence without conversion

The heaviest publisher: 22 posts, mostly explainer carousels closed with a question CTA. Median comments: zero. The questions are being asked into a room where nobody answers, and reels reach a fraction of the 17,855 people who already followed.

Pier 4

The boost experiment

One scenario reel bought its way to 115,860 plays, 185 times the follower base. The account's median is still 4 likes. Reach was rented for a day and none of it stayed. The franchise is worth keeping, the media plan behind it is the question.

The void cohort

Lankin and Marlin Spring

Lankin: ten cross-posts, median 1 like. Marlin Spring: 14,932 followers, the second largest audience on the page, and two posts in thirty days. Between presence without content and content without a channel, roughly 18,600 followers are being left idle.

Your takeaway, any category

Captions and creative are strategy documents your competitors publish for free. Thirty days of them tells you who is native, who is mirroring, and who has stopped thinking about the channel entirely.

These eight frames are served from Apify storage, which is a temporary location. Download them and re-upload to the WordPress media library, then swap the image sources, so the page does not depend on a third party host.

The pattern across eight frames

Eight frames, one pattern, and it inverts the consumer rule. In jewellery the winning frames open on craft or a single human mid moment, and title cards signal bought reach. Here the frame that wins opens on a title card over a crowd, and the two that underperform open on one polished professional in a corporate room. B2B real estate audiences are largely employees, peers and advisors. They respond to rooms full of people they might know, not to production value.

The creative positioning map

Every axis is a measured number. X, paid push: peak plays divided by followers, log scale, how hard the reach was pushed. Y, resonance: median like per play, whether people who saw it responded. Instagram's view counts are unreliable, so plays are the denominator throughout. Hover any circle.

Where the empty space is, the actionable bit

Top right is empty here too: nobody in Canadian private real estate pushes big and lands. Pier 4 pushed 185x and resonance collapsed to 0.02 percent. Skyline lands at 3.3 percent and pushes nothing. The first firm to put media money behind Skyline-grade native creative takes a lane with no competition in it.

Your takeaway, any category

Plot your market on these two axes and the strategy writes itself: never out-spend the bought and bouncing corner. Out-resonate it.

The category story

Native or nothing.

The narrative finding of cycle 001

Canadian private real estate Instagram is a category where the institutions are absent, the mirrors are dying and the natives are winning. Three of eight firms never showed up. Lankin mirrors LinkedIn word for word and earns a median of one like. Skyline, the only firm producing for the platform, out-resonates an audience eight times its size by sixteen to one.

The one attempt to buy the gap closed, Pier 4's 185x reel, rented a day of reach and left the account's median exactly where it was. On this platform, in this category, distribution cannot be bought and cannot be mirrored. It has to be made.

Your takeaway, any category

One brand changing message is noise. A whole cohort absent, a mirror failing and a native winning in the same window is the market telling you what the channel requires. Watch the pattern, not the post.

The cautionary tale

Posting into the void.

Lankin Investments, 3,681 followers, median 1 like

Ten posts inside the window. Four scored zero likes and zero comments. The account is not dormant. It publishes on schedule, and the copy is identical to the LinkedIn posts that pull 67 and 103 likes on the same firm's other channel.

This is not a content quality problem. It is a cross posting problem: LinkedIn native long form copy pasted into a visual feed, where it dies. The same firm proves the copy works elsewhere.

Your takeaway, any category

If the median response to your last ten posts is statistically zero, pausing the channel is not a risk. It is a diagnosis.

Frequently asked

Why only five accounts?

Because only five of the eight firms tracked in the LinkedIn index have an Instagram account. Starlight, Centurion and Hazelview have none, verified against their own websites and by direct search. The index reports what exists rather than padding the table.

How is this scored differently from the LinkedIn index?

Instagram publishes plays, so Resonance can use like per play and Distribution honesty can use peak plays divided by followers, where lower is better. LinkedIn publishes neither, so its index substitutes share to like. They are deliberately different instruments and the scores are not comparable across the two pages.

Why like per play rather than like per view?

Instagram's view counts are inconsistently reported and sometimes lower than like counts, which produces impossible ratios. Plays are reported reliably, so every resonance number on this page uses plays as the denominator and says so.

Is any of the data estimated?

No. Every number comes from public Instagram data at scrape time, and every claim links to its source post.
