Med Spa Index

What five selected Ontario med spas posted on Instagram, the public response those posts received, and what another clinic could learn from it. A side-by-side reading of VitalityMD, Signature Cosmetic Clinic, Laserbody MD, Skin Vitality and SpaMedica.

What stood out

Three findings from 32 posts published on the four accounts that could be read, over the 30 days from 15 September to 14 October 2026. When the logged-out collection tools requested Skin Vitality’s profile, Instagram returned a 21+ viewing restriction, so it could not be read; it is shown, not analysed, and not treated as inactive.

1. Posting frequency split the four readable accounts into two groups

VitalityMD published 17 posts and Signature Cosmetic Clinic 11 (10 of its own plus 1 collaboration post) in the window. SpaMedica published 3 and Laserbody MD 1, although Laserbody MD has the largest following of the four readable accounts (25,645). Denominator: all 32 posts on the four readable feeds in the window; coverage reached the window start for all four.

2. More reels did not mean more plays per reel

Reels were 19 of the 31 posts the four accounts published themselves. VitalityMD published 13 reels with a median of 444 recorded plays each; Signature Cosmetic Clinic published 4 with a median of 5,748.5, about 12.9 times higher. Even Signature Cosmetic Clinic’s lowest reel (4,280 plays) recorded more than VitalityMD’s highest (981). Denominator: own reels with a recorded play count, 13 and 4. Plays count views, not unique people, and public data cannot show whether any reel was boosted with paid budget. The two accounts post about different subjects (see “Before comparing the numbers” under the comparison table), so this is not a like-for-like comparison.

3. Partner recognition drew the most likes where likes could be compared

At both accounts where likes were visible on every own post and there was more than one post, the most-liked post marked an award from a treatment manufacturer: Signature Cosmetic Clinic’s Solta Medical award (73 likes, 1.8 times its median) and SpaMedica’s Galderma Sculptra recognition (38 likes, 5.4 times its median). Both captions tag the manufacturer’s account; public counts do not show who liked a post, so whether those likes came from the clinic’s own followers is not known. Denominator: 10 and 3 own posts; two accounts is too few to call this a pattern.

What this means for your clinic

Follower count and reel count describe an account’s size and output; in this group, neither lined up with plays or likes per post. Ask your marketing team for one monthly line per format: posts published, median plays or likes per post, and how many enquiries mentioned Instagram, so volume is never mistaken for response.

Account by account

For each account: what it published, my interpretation, a limitation or another explanation, and one test. Observations come from the posts; interpretations are mine and are labelled as such. None of these clinics is an HMM client.

VitalityMD (@vitalitymdtoronto)

What it published. 17 posts: 13 reels and 4 carousels. 12 of the 17 dealt with menopause, hormones, pelvic floor or sexual health, often through a relatable or humorous opening; the rest covered a brain stimulation device, body measurement, a team member’s story and a general welcome message. No caption named an injectable, although its website lists them.

Interpretation. In this window the account’s posts centred on women’s health rather than the wider service list on its website. Several reels opened with a relatable or humorous line; whether that opening changed how people responded is not shown by these counts.

Limitation. Likes were hidden on 13 of 17 posts, so response can only be read through plays and comments. Plays per reel were modest (444 median) and comments rare (0 median); people may respond to private topics by message rather than in public comments, but public data cannot show messages, so this is untested.

One test. Run the same symptom topic as two reels, one with a humorous opening and one with a plain question, two weeks apart; compare plays, shares and saves from Instagram Insights.

What this means for your clinic

A focused topic list is one way to run an account; this data cannot show whether it brings more enquiries than a broad menu. If your posts cover sensitive topics, ask your team to count direct messages and calls per post as well as comments, so the response you can see is not the only one you judge.

Signature Cosmetic Clinic (@signature_cosmetic_clinic)

What it published. 10 own posts (4 reels, 4 carousels, 2 static images) and 1 collaboration post owned by @signature_cosmetic_lana. The mix was wide: treatment timing prompts for skin tightening and pigmentation, an upcoming skincare event, an open house recap, two patient result images, a festival greeting, a credited fashion week clip and the manufacturer award.

Interpretation. Reels had the highest median likes at this account: 57 for reels, 40 for carousels and 7.5 for the two static result images, which drew no more than 8 likes each. Several treatment reels used a seasonal prompt (results in time for the holidays); whether that prompt affected plays cannot be separated from topic or format here.

Limitation. The credited fashion week clip recorded 4,280 plays without being about a treatment, so topic alone does not explain plays at this account. Paid boosting cannot be seen in public data, and the result images were single photos, so format and content are mixed together.

One test. Publish the next patient result as a short reel instead of a still image, with the same consent and caption, and compare likes and plays with the account’s reel median.

What this means for your clinic

Here the two still result images drew fewer likes than the account’s reels and carousels. Two images are an observation, not a rule. Ask your team whether your result posts are judged by their own numbers, and test a second format before producing more of one.

Laserbody MD (@laserbodymd)

What it published. One post in the window: a reel for Women’s History Month featuring its team, pinned at the top of the profile. The previous post was on 13 July 2026. Earlier in the year the same feed carried 74 posts between February and June.

Observed gap. Before this reel, the account’s previous post was on 13 July 2026, 80 days earlier. The reason for the gap is not known from public data. The account still had the largest following of the four readable accounts at capture.

Limitation. One post supports no conclusion about what works for this account. Public data cannot show whether the gap was planned, and nothing here shows any effect on enquiries or bookings.

One test. If restarting, publish a fixed weekly schedule for eight weeks and compare profile visits and website taps in Insights with the eight weeks before.

What this means for your clinic

Post dates are visible to anyone who opens your profile. Ask your team whether any gap in the last six months was planned, and what the most recent posts on your grid show today.

Skin Vitality (@skinvitality)

What could be read. Only the profile header: the account exists and is linked from the brand’s website, which lists 14 Ontario clinics. Instagram returned a 21+ viewing restriction when the logged-out collection tools I used requested the profile (its reply: “You must be 21 years old or over to see this profile”), so posts, likes, plays and followers could not be collected.

Interpretation. None about its content or how often it posts. The reason for the restriction is not known from public data.

Limitation. The account was not replaced and is not counted as inactive. A future edition can include it only if its posts become readable to the same tools.

What this means for your clinic

Instagram returned a 21+ viewing restriction when the logged-out collection tools requested Skin Vitality’s profile. Ask your team whether your clinic’s viewing restrictions are intentional and appropriate for its services.

SpaMedica (@spamedicamedspa)

What it published. 3 posts: the Galderma recognition reel, a Thanksgiving image and a carousel on not looking “done”. The two posts before the window were on 4 August 2026 and 24 June 2026.

Interpretation. The “not looking done” carousel addresses a concern about treatment results looking obvious; with 3 posts, these counts cannot show whether that topic resonated. It published 3 posts in the 30 days, fewer than VitalityMD (17) and Signature Cosmetic Clinic (11).

Limitation. With 3 posts, the median of 7 likes rests on almost nothing, and the newest post had about a day to collect likes before collection.

One test. Turn the consultation concern into a short series (one post a week for four weeks) and compare saves and profile visits with the account’s earlier posts.

What this means for your clinic

SpaMedica’s carousel answered one consultation concern: results that look “done”. Try the same with the concern your injectors hear most, as one carousel. Measure its saves in Insights seven days after posting, against the median saves of your last five carousels at seven days.

Patterns across the five

Only patterns the data supports, with their exceptions. None of them shows cause and effect.

Public conversation was thin everywhere. Median comments per own post were between 0 and 3 at all four readable accounts. The most-commented post had 8 comments (Signature Cosmetic Clinic, an event announcement). Comments are not bookings, and public data cannot show messages or calls.

Treatment and condition posts were the largest content type. 17 of 31 own posts centred on a named treatment or condition. Exception: Laserbody MD’s only post and two of SpaMedica’s three were not treatment posts.

Recognition posts had the most likes at the two accounts where likes could be compared. 7 own posts were recognition, team, greeting or third-party content; they include the most-liked post at Signature Cosmetic Clinic and SpaMedica. Exception: VitalityMD’s standout was a treatment reel, chosen by plays because likes were hidden.

Likes were not always public. VitalityMD hid likes on 13 of 17 own posts; the other three showed them on every post. Hidden likes limit what anyone, including competitors, can read from the outside.

What this means for your clinic

Across these four accounts comments were rare, so a quiet comment section alone says little about how a post did. Ask your team to track saves, shares and direct messages per post, since those are the signals your own Insights show and these public counts miss.

What a clinic owner could test

Three proposed experiments, each tied to a finding above. These are suggestions to discuss with your team, not results.

Compare posts at the same age. Likes, plays, saves and shares keep growing after a post goes out, so read each post’s numbers at the same time after publication, for example seven days, in these tests and in the account tests above. Do not compare a one-day-old post with a month-old post; if the ages differ, record each post’s age beside its numbers and name the difference when you compare.

  1. Steady schedule versus your recent pattern (finding 1). Compare: eight weeks at a fixed number of posts per week against the eight weeks before. Measure: profile visits, website link taps and enquiries that mention Instagram, from Insights and your enquiry form.
  2. Two openings for the same treatment reel (finding 2). Compare: one reel that opens with a relatable situation and one that opens with a direct timing prompt, same treatment, a week apart, repeated twice. Measure: plays per reel, average watch time and shares, read against your own reel median rather than another clinic’s.
  3. Recognition and team posts against treatment explainers (finding 3). Compare: over six weeks, pair each recognition or team post with a treatment explainer in the same week. Measure: likes, and also saves, profile visits and messages, to see whether recognition posts are followed by saves, visits or messages, not only likes.

Window, collection and completeness

Window. The most recent 30 complete calendar days before collection, Toronto time: 15 September 2026 to 14 October 2026, both days included.

Collection. On 15 October 2026, follower counts came from the Apify Instagram profile scraper and posts from the Apify Instagram scraper, all five accounts in the same run, with up to 80 recent posts per account and no date filter. The post run returned 321 records. Run caps totalled 2.25 USD; neither run reached its cap.

Completeness. For each readable account, the oldest unpinned post returned was published before 15 September 2026, so the window was covered back to its start. Older pinned posts were excluded by their publication date; a post pinned but published inside the window stays in. 2 records were errors without a post (the Skin Vitality profile, returned with a 21+ viewing restriction, and one restricted page not linked to any cohort account) and were set aside.

Definitions and formulas
  • Own post. A post where the cohort account is the recorded owner. A collaboration post owned by another account is counted in posts in window but not in format, likes, comments or plays, so no post is counted twice. No post was shared between two cohort accounts.
  • Median visible likes. The median of likes on own posts where Instagram showed the count. Hidden likes are excluded and shown as a coverage note (likes visible on N of M posts).
  • Median comments. The median of comment counts on own posts.
  • Median reel plays. The median of the recorded play count on own reels. A play is a view, not a unique person.
  • Times the median. A standout post’s likes (or plays) divided by the account’s median likes (or plays), rounded to one decimal.
  • Not used. No likes-per-follower rate, composite score, weighting or rank: with one to seventeen posts per account, they would suggest more precision than the data has.
  • Caption mentions. A caption names a service category if it contains any of these words: Injectables: botox, filler, sculptra, injectable, neuromodulator, dysport, juvederm, skinvive, radiesse, injection; RF microneedling: morpheus, potenza, scarlet, rf microneedling; Body contouring: coolsculpt, emsculpt, bodyfx, sculpsure, body contour; Medical weight loss: semaglutide, ozempic, wegovy, mounjaro, tirzepatide, glp-1; Laser hair removal: laser hair; IV therapy: iv therapy, iv drip, nad+, iv lounge.
Collaborations, missing data and what is not measured

Collaborations. 1 collaboration post appeared on a readable feed in the window (Signature Cosmetic Clinic, owned by @signature_cosmetic_lana). No standout post was co-authored; two standout captions tag a manufacturer account; whether that tag brought views or likes from the manufacturer’s followers is not shown by public counts.

Missing data. Hidden likes (13 own posts) and the account behind the 21+ viewing restriction are recorded as missing, never as zero.

Not measured. Website quality, search rankings, reviews, regulatory matters, ad spend, paid distribution, leads, consultations and revenue. Engagement is not bookings.

Questions readers ask

Which med spas are in the Med Spa Index?

This edition compares five selected Ontario med spa brands: VitalityMD, Signature Cosmetic Clinic, Laserbody MD, Skin Vitality and SpaMedica. Instagram returned a 21+ viewing restriction for Skin Vitality’s profile to the logged-out collection tools, so it could not be read.

Does the Med Spa Index rank clinics?

No. It compares public Instagram activity on a few measures, each with its own coverage. There is no score, no rank and no claim about clinical quality.

Do likes, comments or reel plays show bookings?

No. They are public signals of attention. A play is a view, not a unique person, and none of these figures shows enquiries, consultations or revenue.

Can I compare this edition with earlier ones?

No. Editions 001 and 002 studied different, larger sets of Greater Toronto Area accounts over different windows. Edition 003 is a deliberately selected five-account comparison, so differences from earlier editions are not changes in performance.
