How automation works

From trigger to outcome.

  1. Trigger

    A call, form, message or booking comes in.

  2. Action

    The system replies, sorts it and updates your tools.

  3. Follow-up

    Reminders and next steps are sent automatically based on the rules and timing you set.

  4. Outcome

    More enquiries answered, and fewer slip through.

Automation handles the routine steps. People still handle the conversations that need a person.

Example workflow

A new enquiry comes in

  1. Someone submits a website form
  2. The enquiry is recorded and you’re notified
  3. An appropriate confirmation is sent
  4. You follow up with the details already in front of you

Why it helps: Less manual copying and a clearer path from enquiry to follow-up.

Example workflow

An appointment is booked

  1. A customer books a time online
  2. The booking is added to your calendar and CRM
  3. A confirmation goes out, then a reminder before the visit
  4. A no-show or cancellation triggers a rebooking message

Why it helps: Fewer missed appointments and less time spent on reminder calls.

Example workflow

A customer asks a question

  1. A question arrives by chat or message
  2. The assistant answers if it’s a supported question
  3. Anything uncertain is passed to a person with the context
  4. The conversation is logged for review

Why it helps: Common questions get answered quickly, and people handle the rest.

Illustrative workflow patterns, not live automations or client results.

Common problems

Where time and enquiries get lost.

  • Enquiries wait for business hours

    What to improve: instant first replies by chat, message or phone, with a person taking over when needed.

  • The same questions, answered by hand

    What to improve: an assistant trained on your services, prices and policies.

  • Leads go quiet after the first contact

    What to improve: automatic follow-up and reminders that run on a schedule.

  • Copying details between tools

    What to improve: forms, inbox, calendar and CRM connected, so data moves on its own.

Automation

  • Follows set rules and triggers
  • Sends confirmations and reminders
  • Updates CRM records and notifications
  • Best for predictable, repeatable tasks

AI assistant

  • Interprets supported questions or information
  • Helps answer common customer questions
  • Routes uncertain enquiries to a person
  • Useful where language or judgement is involved

Not every business problem needs AI. Sometimes a simple automated workflow is the better answer.

Questions

Before you start.

Is this a good fit for me?+

A good fit if

  • You get enquiries that wait or go unanswered
  • Your team answers the same questions again and again
  • You use tools like a CRM, calendar or booking system, or are ready to

Maybe not the right fit if

  • You want AI to replace every human conversation
  • You don’t yet get regular enquiries to automate
  • You want a tool switched on with no time spent on setup
Will it sound robotic?+

It shouldn’t. I write the replies in your voice and train the assistant on your own services and policies, then review real conversations and adjust.

What happens when the AI can’t answer?+

It hands the conversation to you or your team, with the details already collected. I set the rules for when that happens before anything goes live.

How is this different from an off-the-shelf chatbot?+

It’s built around your business: your questions, your booking flow and the tools you already use, rather than a generic script.

How long does it take to set up?+

It depends on how many tools are involved and how much needs writing. I’ll give you a timeline once I’ve seen your setup, and a simple first version can usually go live before the full system.
