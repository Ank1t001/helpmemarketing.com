How much does digital marketing cost in Canada?

Compare Canadian pricing, what each service includes and the extra costs to budget for.

AK
Ankit Kumar 12 min read Updated November 2026

Digital marketing costs in Canada depend on the services, workload and support you need. For initial budgeting, allow around CAD $600 to $1,500 a month for local SEO, $400 to $1,500+ for Google Ads management, or $1,500 to $7,500+ for combined marketing support. These are indicative budgeting estimates informed by selected Canadian provider prices, not national averages. Your quote will depend on the scope. For paid campaigns, the provider’s fee is separate from the money you spend on ads.

Key Takeaways

  • Compare the work included, not just the monthly fee.
  • Check whether advertising spend, creative production and setup cost extra.
  • Choose a scope you can sustain and measure.
  • Ask who will do the work, what you will receive and how progress will be reviewed.

What are you paying for?

The same label can describe very different work. One “social media package” is a handful of scheduled posts; another includes video production and replying to comments. For each service, these are the questions that explain most of the difference in price.

SEO

  • What it covers: Improving how your site appears in search: technical fixes, page content, local listings and links, with regular reporting.
  • What changes the price: The number of locations and pages, how competitive your search terms are, how much new content is produced, and whether fixes are implemented or only recommended.
  • What may cost extra: Website development to make the fixes, extra articles, and SEO software.
  • Ask: “Does the fee cover implementing recommendations, or only identifying them?”

See what my SEO service covers.

Paid ads management (Google and Meta)

  • What it covers: Building and running campaigns: targeting, budgets, bids, ad testing and reporting.
  • What changes the price: The number of campaigns and platforms, the size of the ad budget, how much creative is needed, and whether tracking has to be built.
  • What may cost extra: The ad spend itself, a setup fee, landing pages, creative production and call or conversion tracking tools.
  • Ask: “Does the quote separate management, ad spend, creative and tracking work?”

The ad spend is separate from the management fee. By default Google bills your own payment method for advertising costs. For Meta, budget separately for images and video, which are often outside the management fee. See how I run paid ads.

Social media management

  • What it covers: Planning, writing and scheduling posts on agreed platforms, with reporting. Higher budgets add video and community management.
  • What changes the price: The number of platforms, how often you post, how much video is involved, and whether replies and comments are handled.
  • What may cost extra: Photo and video shoots, paid promotion of posts and influencer fees.
  • Ask: “How many posts, videos, revisions and community responses are included?”

See my branding and social service.

Website or landing page

  • What it covers: Designing, building and launching the agreed pages, with basic setup for search and analytics.
  • What changes the price: The number of pages, custom design versus a template, integrations such as booking or e-commerce, and who supplies the copy and images.
  • What may cost extra: Copywriting, photography, hosting, domain renewal and ongoing maintenance.
  • Ask: “Who provides the copy, images, hosting and ongoing maintenance?”

Website projects are quoted by scope. Page count, design, copy and integrations affect the price. See my website service.

Combined marketing support

  • What it covers: Several channels run together, such as SEO, ads and social, with shared planning and reporting.
  • What changes the price: How many channels get active work each month, how much content is produced, and how detailed the reporting is.
  • What may cost extra: Ad spend, photo and video production, and software.
  • Ask: “Which channels receive active work each month, and what is delivered?”

Compare packages by the work delivered, not by the number of services named. A package that lists five channels but delivers little in each can cost more for each useful piece of work than a focused one. Use these points to put two packages side by side.

Freelancer, agency or in-house: what changes the cost?

Each option can be the right one. The difference is less about the hourly rate and more about who does the work, what is covered, and what you need to manage yourself.

OptionPotential fitWhat to check
FreelancerA defined scope or a specialist needRelevant experience, capacity, cover when they are away, and who implements the work
AgencyWork that needs several capabilitiesWho does the work, how involved senior people are, and what the retainer includes
In-houseOngoing work that needs close involvement in the businessSalary and benefits, tools, production support and the specialist skills one hire may not have

Monthly retainer, project fee or percentage of ad spend?

Two providers can price the same work in different ways. Here is what each model covers and what to clarify.

  • Monthly retainer. A fixed fee for an ongoing scope, common for SEO, social media and ads management. It suits continuous work. Clarify exactly what is delivered each month and what happens to unused hours.
  • Project fee. A one-time price for a defined deliverable, such as a website or a tracking setup. It suits work with a clear finish. Clarify what counts as a change in scope and how revisions are charged.
  • Percentage of ad spend. The management fee is a share of the advertising budget, usually with a minimum fee. It suits larger or growing ad budgets. Clarify the percentage, the minimum and the work included.
  • Hourly. Billed for time used, common for consulting and small defined tasks. Ask for an estimate and a cap before work starts.
Illustrative example: at 15% of ad spend with a $500 minimum, a $2,000 monthly budget costs $500 in fees (15% would be $300, so the minimum applies, an effective 25%), while a $10,000 budget costs $1,500. Check the effective rate at your own budget.

Frequently asked questions

How much does digital marketing cost per month in Canada?

For initial budgeting, allow around $600 to $1,500 a month for local SEO, $400 to $1,500 or more for Google Ads management, and $1,500 to $7,500 or more for combined marketing support, all in CAD. These are budgeting estimates informed by selected Canadian provider prices, not averages. Ad spend and taxes are extra.

What does a digital marketing package include?

It depends on the provider. Compare the channels that get active work each month, the deliverables and how often they arrive, who implements changes, who produces content, how reporting works, whether ad spend is included, the minimum term, and who owns the accounts and content.

Is advertising spend included in the agency fee?

Usually not. The management fee pays the provider to run the campaigns. The ad spend goes to Google or Meta, and by default the platform charges your own payment method. Some agencies pay the platforms and re-bill you, so ask which applies before comparing quotes.

What should a small business prioritise with a limited budget?

Start with one channel that matches how your customers already look for you, and do it properly rather than spreading the budget thinly. Make sure enquiries are tracked, so you can see what the spend produces before adding a second channel.

How much does social media management cost?

For budgeting, allow around $800 to $3,000 or more a month in CAD. One Canadian provider publishes $699 a month for full social media management and content packages from $849 to $2,499. The cost rises with more platforms, video and community management. Ask how many posts, videos, revisions and replies are included, and whether shoots and paid promotion are extra.

Is a freelancer cheaper than an agency?

It depends on the scope and experience of the provider. Compare the deliverables, implementation and support included in each quote.

Are setup and tracking charged separately?

Sometimes. Some Canadian providers publish a one-time setup fee for paid ads on top of the monthly fee, and tracking, landing pages or tools can be billed separately. Ask what setup includes and whether conversion tracking is built and tested within the quoted fee.

How should I compare two marketing quotes?

Put them side by side on the same items: the work delivered and how often, costs billed separately, who implements changes, who owns the accounts and content, how results are reported, and the contract terms. The cheaper quote may cover less work, and the dearer one may include things you do not need.
