How the rankings work

Discovery, 30%Average recorded reel plays divided by followers. Plays can include repeat viewing.

Interest, 20%Engagement rate: average likes and comments per post divided by followers.

Consideration, 30%Average comments per post. A comment-based proxy, not a count of enquiries or bookings.

Conversion path, 20%Scored from the caption ask and the number of taps between a post and a booking route, read from captions, bios and links.

Why the leader matters: Mulberry records about a quarter of Hair Loss Lab’s plays per follower (1.04x against 4.30x). It leads because it scores higher on comments per post and on the caption ask, which together carry half the weight.

Brands with fewer than five posts: Wigs R Us Toronto, Toronto Wig Studio and Wig Boutique published fewer than five posts, and Ethereal Ink by Ash none. One or two posts cannot give a stable average, so their scores are shown but not ranked, and they are left out of pooled figures.

First edition: this is edition 001, so there is no movement since a previous edition yet. The scoreboard uses averages, because several brands have few reels and a median would move on a single post.

Mulberry

In frame: A television studio set: a woman in a multicolour crochet mini dress, arms raised, in front of a curved grey sofa and a black and white chevron rug, with Happy Birthday, Melissa! in pink script across the frame.

539 likes and 35,928 plays, the most liked post on the board. Observation: it is not a pop-up announcement, a product or a result, but a named person the audience may already know from daytime television. Possible interpretation: the ambassador’s profile carried the post. Suggested test: a second ambassador post timed with a pop-up announcement.

Truly You

In frame: Two women in summer dresses with conference lanyards beside a tall roll-up banner reading World Trichology Conference, Paris, July 12 2026, sponsor logos along the bottom, a hotel window behind.

131 likes, 8.2 times its median. Observation: its second most liked post is also expert-led, a reel featuring a trichologist at 108, while its summer sale post drew 15 likes and a course announcement 2. Possible interpretation: posts that show its specialists drew more likes than promotions in this sample. Suggested test: pair the next promotion with a named specialist explaining it.

Hair Loss Lab

In frame: A practitioner in a white clinic coat holds a printed teaser poster over their own face: BREAKTHROUGH TECHNOLOGY, COMING AUGUST 1 above a blurred handheld device, with BE BREAKTHROUGH captioned below.

51 likes and 14,299 plays, with no face, result or scalp in the frame. Observation: it opened a four-post launch countdown that drew 2 comments in total. Possible interpretation: the announcement, not the image, carried the post. Suggested test: end the next launch post with a keyword to comment or DM.

Modern Pigment

In frame: A studio-grade poster: a bald, tattooed man on the phone outside a black storefront with the Modern Pigment door decal, FREE CONSULTATIONS in gold, a feature list, and a boxed call to action reading DM CONSULT for your free assessment.

27 likes on 26,280 followers and no comments. Observation: it is the only top-post frame with a keyword ask printed inside the creative. Possible interpretation: a DM ask depends on a local audience that is ready to reply. Suggested test: the same ask on a geo-tagged reel aimed at local clients.

Sudbury Skin Clinique

In frame: A mirror selfie of two aestheticians in scrubs inside a treatment room, white cabinets and a treatment bed behind them, with a casual text overlay in blue block letters across the middle.

19 likes against a median of 6.5. Observation: the first slide shows two staff members, while the caption is an SPF reminder. Suggested test: a staff-led post about its hair loss service.

Wigs R Us Toronto

In frame: A young woman in glasses and a black sweatshirt with long, glossy dark hair, in a boutique with a pink floral wall and rows of blonde wigs on mannequin heads behind her.

13 likes, near its median, on one of two feed posts in 90 days. Possible interpretation: the account may be more active in Stories, which this index cannot see.

Toronto Wig Studio

In frame: A long blonde wig on a white stand on a glass salon trolley, a hand with a pink manicure touching the hair, Davines product jars in the foreground, a blurred salon behind.

12 likes, about twice its median, on a four-post window. It is the only brand with active Meta ads found, four store-visit ads, which do not appear in these feed metrics.

Wig Boutique

In frame: A woman in her fifties in a white top wearing a wavy brunette wig, photographed in a fitting room with a tri-fold mirror, a silver wall clock and grey curtains behind her.

Its only feed post across two accounts in 90 days: 3 likes and 2 comments on a caption that asked a question. With one post, there is nothing to compare it with.

The pattern across eight frames

Seven of the eight top-post frames show a person; only Toronto Wig Studio’s shows the product alone. The two highest multiples of median likes, Mulberry’s ambassador and Truly You’s specialists, show people the audience can name or recognise. This is a pattern among the selected examples, not a comparison of every post with and without people. Suggested test: each brand’s next result post against a post led by a named person from the team.

How this research was produced

Brands and posts. Nine non-surgical hair loss brands with a public Instagram account, chosen as an editorial roster in three groups: Northern Ontario (Hair Loss Lab, Sudbury Skin Clinique, Wig Boutique, Ethereal Ink by Ash), the Greater Toronto Area (Truly You, Modern Pigment, Wigs R Us Toronto, Toronto Wig Studio) and one touring brand (Mulberry). Wig Boutique is counted as one brand across its Sudbury and Sault Ste. Marie accounts, so the board covers ten handles. Sudbury Skin Clinique’s whole feed is included because a local client looking at it sees all of it.

What was collected. For each account: post date, format, likes, comments, plays where Instagram shows them, caption and link, plus the follower count on the collection date. 185 public posts across the ten handles, published between 10 June and 8 September 2026, were collected on 8 September 2026 with automated public-data tools.

Two collections, one window. The scoreboard and the three stage tables use the 8 September collection. The top posts, themes, formats, post log and research sections use a re-collection on 9 September of the same ten handles over the same window, restricted to the 167 brand-authored posts. The re-collection returned 56 Sudbury Skin Clinique posts against 49, so some counts differ by section. Collaborator posts co-authored on a brand feed are reported in “Observed content series and active ads” and left out of every brand figure.

Reporting window. The 90 days ending 8 September 2026, by each post’s timestamp. Pinned posts count only if they were published inside the window.

Calculations. Plays per follower = average recorded plays per reel ÷ followers. Engagement rate = (average likes + average comments per post) ÷ followers × 100. Comments per post = total comments ÷ posts. Comment to like ratio = total comments ÷ total likes × 100. Peak plays ÷ followers = the highest recorded plays on a reel in the window ÷ followers. The scoreboard uses averages; the top-post comparisons, themes and formats use medians.

Score. 30% Discovery (plays per follower) + 20% Interest (engagement rate) + 30% Consideration (comments per post) + 20% Conversion path (the caption ask and the taps between a post and a booking route), rounded to a whole number. Brands with fewer than five posts are shown with their score but not ranked, and are left out of pooled figures. The edition 001 scores are shown as first calculated; the scaling inside each pillar was not recorded with this page, so it is not restated here.

Missing data and co-authored posts. A brand with no reel shows “No video”, never zero. Ethereal Ink by Ash published no posts in the window and has no score. Co-authored posts are reported separately and never added to a brand’s figures.

Advertising. Active ads in Canada were searched in the Meta Ad Library by brand name on 9 September 2026. A page advertising under a different name would be missed. Counts show that an ad is live; they do not show spend, reach or results.

What public data cannot show. Non-follower reach, saves, shares, profile visits and link clicks are visible only to the account owner. This index uses the closest public measures: plays per follower, engagement rate on followers, comments per post and the caption ask. Posting activity is measured on feed posts only, so a brand active in Stories can look quieter here than it is.

Creative review. The opening frame of each brand’s most liked post is described with AI assistance and checked editorially, then hosted on this site. It does not assess the full reel or every carousel slide.

Review. Data collection, calculations and a first draft are prepared with automated tools and AI assistance. Ankit Kumar reviews the findings and approves each edition before it is published. Corrections are recorded in “Coverage and corrections” and in the edition history.

The narrative finding of edition 001

Hair Loss Lab recorded the most plays per follower, 4.30x, and few comments. Mulberry recorded about a quarter of that rate and the most comments per post, many of them replies to its DM booking ask. The score puts Mulberry first because comments per post and the caption ask carry half its weight.

The top posts and the theme table add a second pattern. Seven of the eight top-post frames show a person, and result reveals, the most published theme, sat near the bottom on median likes. One edition is not a pattern; it will be checked against the next edition.

So the suggestion is narrow. In this sample, high plays without a clear next step drew few public replies.

Frequently asked

Why is Mulberry first when Hair Loss Lab records more plays per follower?

The score gives 30% to plays per follower, 20% to engagement rate, 30% to comments per post and 20% to the caption ask. Hair Loss Lab leads on plays per follower; Mulberry leads on comments per post and ends its pop-up announcements with a booking ask. Comments are a public signal, not a count of bookings.

Why averages and not medians?

Edition 001 has one 90-day window and several brands with few reels, so a median would move on a single post. The scoreboard uses averages; the top-post comparisons, themes and formats use medians.

Why are four brands not ranked?

Wigs R Us Toronto, Toronto Wig Studio and Wig Boutique each published fewer than five posts in 90 days, and Ethereal Ink by Ash none. One or two posts cannot give a stable average, so their scores are shown but not ranked, and they are left out of pooled figures. Posting is measured on feed posts only, so a brand active in Stories can look quieter here than it is.

Why are Sudbury Skin Clinique’s skin and body posts included?

Sudbury Skin Clinique offers hair loss treatment in Sudbury, and a local client looking at its account sees the whole feed. The theme table separates its skin and body posts from its hair posts.

Does the index separate paid and organic engagement?

No. Public post metrics do not provide a paid-versus-organic breakdown. Active ads are recorded separately; the 9 September 2026 search found them only for Toronto Wig Studio.

How are the creative examples reviewed?

The creative review examines the opening image or cover of each brand’s post with the most likes. It does not assess the full reel or every carousel slide. Visual observations are interpreted alongside captions and recorded metrics.

Where do the figures come from?

Post counts, follower counts and engagement figures were collected from public Instagram data on 8 September 2026 for the scoreboard and re-collected on 9 September for the rest of the page. Ratios and scores are calculated from those observations, and the post log links to each source post. Ad counts come from a Meta Ad Library name search in Canada on 9 September. The top-post frames are hosted on this site.
