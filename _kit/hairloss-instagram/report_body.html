The largest audience drew the fewest replies

Toronto Hair Transplant Surgeons has the most followers on the board, 16,975, and its 28 posts averaged 2.4 likes. 26 of the 28 drew no comments. The Meta Ad Library search found 12 of its ads running.

How this research was produced

Clinics and posts. The ten Ontario hair loss clinics with the largest Instagram accounts, chosen on 16 October 2026 by follower count from 25 candidate clinics whose own website links to their Instagram account, counting only accounts that posted in the previous 90 days. Medical, non-surgical and hair transplant clinics are included; wig shops, extension salons and pigment artists are not, nor are US corporate accounts or surgeons’ personal accounts. The clinics fall into two groups: six that offer hair transplant surgery and four non-surgical clinics. Each clinic has one handle.

What was collected. For each account: post date, format, likes, comments, plays where Instagram shows them, caption and link, plus the follower count on the collection date. 167 public posts across the ten handles were collected on 16 October 2026 with automated public-data tools; 147 of them are clinic-authored posts inside the window. Co-authored posts by another account are reported in “Observed content series and active ads” and left out of every clinic figure.

One collection. Edition 002 uses one collection, on 16 October 2026, for every section of this page.

Reporting window. The 90 days ending 16 October 2026 (18 July to 16 October), by each post’s timestamp. Pinned posts count only if they were published inside the window.

Calculations. Plays per follower = average recorded plays per reel ÷ followers (0 for a clinic with no reel). Engagement rate = (average likes + average comments per post) ÷ followers × 100. Comments per post = total comments ÷ posts. Comment to like ratio = total comments ÷ total likes × 100. Peak plays ÷ followers = the highest recorded plays on a reel in the window ÷ followers. The scoreboard uses averages; the top posts, themes and formats use medians.

Score. Score = 30% Discovery + 20% Interest + 30% Consideration + 20% Conversion path, rounded to a whole number. Discovery = percentile of plays per follower (average recorded plays per reel divided by followers; 0 for a clinic with no reel). Interest = percentile of engagement rate ((average likes + average comments per post) divided by followers x 100). Consideration = percentile of average comments per post. Percentiles are taken within the ranked clinics and give half credit to ties; a clinic that is not ranked is placed against the ranked clinics’ values. Conversion path = caption ask (booking 50, general 25, none 0) + steps to enquire (one 50, two 35, three 20, no public route 0). Ranked: five or more clinic-authored posts in the window; clinics with fewer are shown with their score but not ranked and are left out of pooled figures; a clinic with no posts has no score. Score badge: 50 and above high, 20 to 49 middle, below 20 low.

What changed in edition 002. Edition 002 uses one collection and the coded score above, and it starts a new roster, so its scores are not comparable with edition 001’s. There is no movement to show until edition 003.

Missing data and co-authored posts. A clinic with no reel shows “no video”, never zero; CADMEN Clinic posted no reels in the window. Co-authored posts are reported separately and never added to a clinic’s figures.

Advertising. Active ads in Canada were searched in the Meta Ad Library by clinic name on 16 October 2026, up to 25 results a search. Only ads run by the clinic’s own page are counted, so counts are minimums and 25 means the search hit its limit. A page advertising under a different name would be missed. Counts show that an ad is live; they do not show spend, reach or results.

What public data cannot show. Non-follower reach, saves, shares, profile visits and link clicks are visible only to the account owner. This index uses the closest public measures: plays per follower, engagement rate on followers, comments per post and the caption ask. Posting activity is measured on feed posts only, so a clinic active in Stories can look quieter here than it is.

Creative review. The opening frame of each clinic’s most liked post is described with AI assistance and checked editorially, then hosted on this site. It does not assess the full reel or every carousel slide.

Review. Data collection, calculations and a first draft are prepared with automated tools and AI assistance. Ankit Kumar reviews the findings and approves each edition before it is published. Corrections are recorded in “Coverage and corrections” and in the edition history.

The narrative finding of edition 002

Eight of the ten clinics put a booking ask on at least a quarter of their posts, yet 100 of the 147 posts drew no comments. The posts that did draw comments were mostly partnership, team and event posts: the Toronto FC signing (31), a partnership thank-you on Truly You (17) and an event recap on Alopecia & Beyond (13).

The score puts Truly You first because it combines the most plays per follower of the ranked clinics with a booking ask on most posts. The Toronto Hair Transplant Clinic draws more comments and engagement but carried no booking ask in this window.

So the suggestion is narrow. In this sample, a booking ask alone drew few public replies; comments gathered on posts about people and partners.

Frequently asked

Why is Truly You first when The Toronto Hair Transplant Clinic draws more comments?

The score gives 30% to plays per follower, 20% to engagement rate, 30% to comments per post and 20% to the caption ask and route. The Toronto Hair Transplant Clinic leads the ranked clinics on comments per post and engagement rate, most of its comments on one partnership post; Truly You leads on plays per follower and puts a booking ask on most posts. Comments are a public signal, not a count of bookings.

Why averages and not medians?

Several clinics have few reels in the 90-day window, so a median would move on a single post. The scoreboard uses averages; the top-post comparisons, themes and formats use medians.

Why are three clinics not ranked?

Alopecia & Beyond published 3 posts in 90 days, and Hair Transplant Centre Toronto and Hair Transplant Medical Centre 2 each. A few posts cannot give a stable average, so their scores are shown but not ranked, and they are left out of pooled figures. Posting is measured on feed posts only, so a clinic active in Stories can look quieter here than it is.

Why did the clinics change from edition 001?

Edition 001 followed nine non-surgical hair loss brands, mostly wig, extension and pigment studios. From edition 002 the Index follows the ten Ontario hair loss clinics with the largest Instagram accounts, chosen by follower count from clinics whose own website links to their account. Edition 001 stays available as a snapshot.

Does the index separate paid and organic engagement?

No. Public post metrics do not provide a paid-versus-organic breakdown. Active ads are recorded separately; the 16 October 2026 search found them for five of the ten clinics.

How are the creative examples reviewed?

The creative review examines the opening image or cover of each clinic’s post with the most likes. It does not assess the full reel or every carousel slide. Visual observations are interpreted alongside captions and recorded metrics.

Where do the figures come from?

Post counts, follower counts and engagement figures were collected from public Instagram data on 16 October 2026. Ratios and scores are calculated from those observations, and the post log links to each source post. Ad counts come from a Meta Ad Library name search in Canada on 16 October 2026. The top-post frames are hosted on this site.
