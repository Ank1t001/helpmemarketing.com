How to read the comparison

Median likes and comments
The middle result across eligible posts, reducing the influence of one unusually popular post.
Likes relative to followers
Visible likes in relation to audience size. It does not measure how many followers saw the post.
Plays relative to followers
Recorded video plays in relation to audience size. It does not identify unique viewers or paid distribution.

Public engagement does not establish investor interest, leads or revenue.

Equiton, 18,643 followers, 14 posts, 12 median likes

Paid strategy compared

Active ads in the Meta Ad Library in Canada on 2 November 2026, read for each firm’s advertiser pages. The library shows that an ad is live; it does not show spend, reach or results.

Five firms throughout. Since 2 November 2026 the index compares Equiton, Skyline, Lankin Investments, Greybrook Realty Partners and Centurion Asset Management in every section. What stood out

  • Three of the five firms had investor ads running in Canada on 2 November 2026. No active ads matched Greybrook, and Centurion’s four were recruitment ads.
  • Each ran a different message. Equiton’s most common was its luxury development offering (21 of 49 investor ads), headlined with a targeted return; Lankin’s was registered accounts at year-end, RRSP and TFSA (15 of 36); Skyline promoted its funds one by one (15 of 16).
  • Return figures appear in 78% of Lankin’s and 51% of Equiton’s investor ads, and in none of Skyline’s, which describe “historically stable returns” instead.
  • Most ads send people to a Meta link (fb.me) rather than the firm’s site: all 16 of Skyline’s and 43 of Equiton’s 49. 4 of Skyline’s ads name a town (Oakville or Orangeville).
  • Equiton also ran 1 ad for a home sale and Skyline Living ran 6 resident leasing ads; neither is counted as investor-facing.

Company posts and advertising activity

Active advertising alongside modest visible engagement in this sample.

The narrative finding of this edition

The feed alone does not show it: three of the five firms had investor ads running on Meta on 2 November 2026, 108 active ads between Equiton, Lankin and Skyline Wealth Management. Greybrook had none, and Centurion’s four ads recruit staff. The index does not measure ad spend or paid campaign results.

The ads and the company feeds show different things. Skyline Wealth Management, an advertiser page separate from Skyline’s corporate Instagram account, started its 16 investor ads between 6 and 9 October while Skyline’s own feed posted leasing milestones and a charity golf day. Equiton’s ads lead with a luxury condo development and targeted returns, while its feed ran market round-ups and lifestyle reels.

So the suggestion is narrow. In this sample, community posts drew the most likes per post and market commentary the least. The exceptions on the same board are named in the theme table, and one edition is not a pattern. It will be checked against the next edition.

Recommendations for the five firms

One test per firm, from the website, organic and paid comparisons of 2 November 2026. Each is a proposal to test, not a promise of better results.

Equiton

Paid Social and Organic Social

What I saw
Its ads lead with offerings and targeted returns (49 investor ads read), while 1 of its 14 posts named a fund.
Recommendation
Bring one ad message into the feed, for example the apartment fund explainer as a reel, and compare its likes and comments with similar market posts. Read the website by hand to complete the comparison.

Skyline

Paid Social and Website Messaging

What I saw
None of its 16 ads states a return figure, only one names the $50,000 minimum that its own website shows, and every one sends people to a Meta link.
Recommendation
Test a version of one fund ad that states the minimum and who can invest, as the website already does, against the current version, and compare enquiries per ad.

Lankin Investments

Paid Social and Website Messaging

What I saw
Its ads cite a 12% to 16% targeted net return; its homepage lists 10% to 14% and 10% to 12% for two funds.
Recommendation
Make sure the page each ad sends people to names the fund behind the figure in the ad, then compare the RRSP and TFSA year-end messages on enquiries.

Greybrook Realty Partners

Organic Social

What I saw
No active ads were found. Its sponsorship and community posts drew the most likes; its fund post drew 1.
Recommendation
Test telling the U.S. Multifamily fund story through the team and the communities it already posts about, and link that post to the fund page.

Centurion Asset Management

Website Messaging and Paid Social

What I saw
No corporate Instagram account was found, its four active ads recruit staff, and its homepage leads with its Chief Economist’s research.
Recommendation
Test promoting one research article to investors, sending readers to the article, and measure visits from there to the REIT page.

How this research was produced

Firms and posts. Five Canadian private real estate investment firms, chosen as an editorial roster (founder, 2 November 2026): Equiton, Skyline and Lankin (Canadian residential funds), Greybrook Realty Partners (development-led) and Centurion Asset Management. Firms with a corporate Instagram account are scored; Centurion has none that could be found, so it is compared on its website and advertising only. Every company post published in the reporting window is included. Wealthsimple appears as an out-of-category reference and is never scored or pooled.

What was collected. For each account: post date, format, likes, comments, plays where Instagram shows them, caption and link, plus the follower count on the collection date. Data covers the 90 days to the end of the window and was collected for every account on 2 November 2026 with automated public-data tools (Apify, apify/instagram-scraper). A post that hides its like count is recorded as hidden, not as zero.

Reporting window. The last {{WINDOW_DAYS}} days ending {{WINDOW_END_LONG}}, by each post’s timestamp. Pinned posts count only if they were published inside the window. The 90-day data is used only for the 90-day peak.

Calculations. Median likes relative to followers (called engagement rate in earlier editions) = median likes per post ÷ followers × 100, using the posts that show their like counts. Comment-to-like ratio = total comments ÷ total likes × 100, both on the posts that show their likes. Video like ratio = the median, across posts with video, of likes ÷ recorded plays × 100. Peak play ratio = the highest recorded plays on a post in the window ÷ followers.

HMM score. 45% median likes relative to followers + 30% comment-to-like ratio + 25% peak plays relative to followers, rounded to a whole number (earlier editions called these Resonance, Intent and Distribution, first Distribution honesty). The likes component is the firm’s percentile of median likes divided by followers among eligible firms (those with five or more posts). The peak plays component is the percentile of peak plays divided by followers with lower ratios ranked higher; a firm with no video has a ratio of zero and so ranks highest on it. A percentile counts the eligible firms below the value plus half of any ties, divided by the number of eligible firms, × 100. The comment-to-like component scores the ratio, counted on posts that show their likes, in bands: 0 with no comments; below 1.5%, rising in proportion from 0 to 70; from 1.5% to 5%, rising from 70 to 100; above 5%, falling by 10 points per percentage point, to a minimum of 40. Firms with fewer than five posts are left out of the percentile pool and their score is capped at 30. Equal scores are ordered by median likes relative to followers. The method has not changed since edition 003, and that edition’s five published scores were reproduced exactly with it.

Missing data and co-authored posts. A firm with no video shows “No video”, never zero; under the current method that gives a favourable Distribution result, which this page discloses wherever it matters. A co-authored post counts for the firm’s account; the co-author’s follower count is reported separately and never added to the firm’s.

Advertising. Frequently asked

Have the firms included changed?

Yes. On 2 November 2026 the board became Equiton, Skyline, Lankin, Greybrook Realty Partners and Centurion Asset Management; Pier 4 and McGillivray Capital left it. Every account was re-collected that day for the same window, and the edition 005 snapshot keeps the first publication. Changes to the firms included affect percentile-based scores and rankings, so scores are not compared across the change.

Does the index separate paid and organic engagement?

No. Public post metrics do not provide a verified paid-versus-organic breakdown. Active ads are recorded separately from the post metrics.

Why does Greybrook have the highest HMM score?

Greybrook’s comment-to-like ratio is the only one inside HMM’s historical reference band of 1.5 to 5%, which gives it the highest result on that component, and its median likes relative to followers are second to Skyline’s. The score uses HMM’s editorial weights; other metrics order the firms differently. Four of its 13 posts hide their like counts, so its like-based figures use the nine posts that show them; the page says so wherever it matters.

Why is Centurion not in the HMM Ranking?

No corporate Instagram account could be found on 2 November 2026: centurion.ca links none, four likely handles do not exist and a name search found only an unrelated account. Centurion is compared on its website and its Meta advertising instead.

Why is Wealthsimple on the page if it is not a real estate firm?

As a reference only. It is never pooled into any median, percentile or score, it does not appear in the HMM Ranking, and its rows in the downloadable CSV are tagged REFERENCE.

Which metrics use followers and which use plays?

The HMM score uses median likes divided by followers. The video comparison uses likes divided by recorded plays. These are different measures.

How are the creative examples reviewed?

The creative review examines the opening image or cover of each firm’s post with the most likes. It does not assess the full reel or every carousel slide. Visual observations are interpreted alongside captions and recorded metrics. Where a frame cannot be read, this page labels it unreviewed and does not describe it.

Where do the figures come from?

Post counts, follower counts and available engagement figures were collected from public sources on 2 November 2026 for the window ending 13 October 2026. Ratios and index scores are calculated from those observations. Ad counts are the Meta Ad Library’s totals on the same day, by the advertiser page that placed them, and unavailable values remain blank. The post log includes source links.
