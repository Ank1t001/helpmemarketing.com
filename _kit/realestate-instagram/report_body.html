How the rankings work

Resonance, 45%Each firm’s median likes per post divided by its follower count, ranked relative to the other eligible firms.

Intent, 30%Total comments divided by total likes, scored using the index’s published bands. Ratios above 5% receive a lower score. This is a comment-based proxy, not a measure of investor intent.

Distribution, 25%Peak plays divided by followers, ranked relative to eligible firms. Lower ratios receive higher scores under the current method. Earlier editions called this pillar Distribution honesty.

Why the leader matters: With no video in the window, Pier 4 receives a favourable Distribution result under the current method. This contributes to its overall lead.

A small comment sample: 54 comments were recorded across 57 posts. Lankin received 27, McGillivray Capital 21, Pier 4 five, Equiton one and Skyline none. Small differences can move the rankings.

Movement since the previous edition: Changes compare with the edition 004 scoreboard dated 5 October 2026. Its raw posts were unavailable for recalculation, so the comparison uses its listed scores and ranks. The scoring method has not changed since edition 003.

How this research was produced

Firms and posts. Five Canadian private real estate investment firms with a corporate Instagram account, chosen as an editorial roster in two groups: mid market fund (Skyline, Equiton, Lankin) and emerging and boutique (Pier 4, McGillivray Capital). Every company post published in the reporting window is included. Wealthsimple appears as an out-of-category reference and is never scored or pooled.

What was collected. For each account: post date, format, likes, comments, plays where Instagram shows them, caption and link, plus the follower count on the collection date. Data covers the previous 90 days and was collected on 13 October 2026 with automated public-data tools.

Reporting window. The last 30 days ending 13 October 2026, by each post’s timestamp. Pinned posts count only if they were published inside the window. The 90-day data is used only for the 90-day peak.

Calculations. Engagement rate = median likes per post ÷ followers × 100. Comment ratio = total comments ÷ total likes × 100. Video like ratio = the median, across posts with video, of likes ÷ recorded plays × 100. Peak play ratio = the highest recorded plays on a post in the window ÷ followers.

Score. 45% Resonance + 30% Intent + 25% Distribution, rounded to a whole number. Resonance is the firm’s engagement-rate percentile among eligible firms (those with five or more posts). Distribution is the percentile of the peak play ratio with lower ratios ranked higher; a firm with no video has a ratio of zero and so ranks highest on Distribution. A percentile counts the eligible firms below the value plus half of any ties, divided by the number of eligible firms, × 100. Intent scores the comment ratio in bands: 0 with no comments; below 1.5%, rising in proportion from 0 to 70; from 1.5% to 5%, rising from 70 to 100; above 5%, falling by 10 points per percentage point, to a minimum of 40. Firms with fewer than five posts are left out of the percentile pool and their score is capped at 30. Equal scores are ranked by engagement rate. The method has not changed since edition 003, and that edition’s five published scores were reproduced exactly with it.

Missing data and co-authored posts. A firm with no video shows “No video”, never zero; under the current method that gives a favourable Distribution result, which this page discloses wherever it matters. A co-authored post counts for the firm’s account; the co-author’s follower count is reported separately and never added to the firm’s.

Advertising. Active ads in Canada were searched in the Meta Ad Library by exact phrase for each firm’s name and its known founder advertiser on the collection date, then grouped by the advertiser page that placed them. Counts are minimums and do not show spend, reach or results.

Creative review. The opening frame or cover of each firm’s most liked post is described with AI assistance and checked editorially. Frames that cannot be read are labelled unreviewed and not described.

Review. Data collection, calculations and a first draft are prepared with automated tools and AI assistance. Ankit Kumar reviews the findings and approves each edition before it is published. Corrections are recorded in “Coverage, previous-edition comparison and correction” and in the edition history.

The narrative finding of edition 005

This edition adds a fact the feed alone could not show: active Meta ads were found for all five firms and associated advertiser pages, at least 72 investor-facing ads between them. The index does not measure ad spend or paid campaign results.

The ads and the company feeds show different things. Skyline Wealth Management, an advertiser page separate from Skyline’s corporate Instagram account, started 16 investor ads between 6 and 9 October while Skyline’s own feed posted leasing milestones and a charity golf day. Pier 4’s advertiser page has run investor ads since June, many placed on Instagram only, while its corporate feed published no reel in this window.

So the suggestion is narrow. In this sample, posts about people and community drew the most likes per post and market commentary the least. The exceptions on the same board are named in the theme table above, and one edition is not a pattern. It will be checked against the next edition.

How the ad counts were taken

Active ads in Canada were searched by exact phrase for each firm’s name and its known founder advertiser, then grouped by the advertiser page that placed them. Counts are minimums: a keyword search misses ads that never name the firm, and one search returned its maximum of 25 results. Resident leasing pages are listed separately from investor-facing pages. The library shows that an ad is live; it does not show budget, reach or results.

Public posts and ad listings reveal parts of a competitor’s activity, rather than a complete campaign calendar.

Frequently asked

Have the firms included changed?

No. The five firms are the same as edition 003: Skyline, Equiton, Lankin, Pier 4 and McGillivray Capital. Marlin Spring left at edition 003 after two posts in each of three consecutive reporting windows. Changes to the firms included can affect percentile-based scores and rankings, and the page will say so.

Does the index separate paid and organic engagement?

No. Public post metrics do not provide a verified paid-versus-organic breakdown. Active ads are recorded separately from the post metrics.

Why is Pier 4 first with no video in the window?

Pier 4’s result partly reflects the current method’s treatment of accounts without video. Its Distribution component receives a favourable result despite having no measured video plays in this window. The same treatment applied to Lankin in edition 003. If Pier 4’s Distribution were scored at the middle of the pool instead, it would fall behind McGillivray Capital. That sensitivity is published rather than changing the method mid series.

Why is McGillivray Capital’s plays-to-followers ratio so high?

Its top reel recorded 43,066 plays against 646 firm followers, approximately 66.7x. The reel was co-authored with the founder’s larger account (@scott_mcgillivray, 351,567 followers). Public data does not establish the share of plays attributable to each audience or to paid distribution.

Why is Wealthsimple on the page if it is not a real estate firm?

As a reference only. It is never pooled into any median, percentile or score, it does not appear on the scoreboard, and its rows in the downloadable CSV are tagged REFERENCE.

Which metrics use followers and which use plays?

The score’s Resonance component uses median likes divided by followers. The video comparison uses likes divided by recorded plays. These are different measures.

How are the creative examples reviewed?

The creative review examines the opening image or cover of each firm’s post with the most likes. It does not assess the full reel or every carousel slide. Visual observations are interpreted alongside captions and recorded metrics. Where a frame cannot be read, this page labels it unreviewed and does not describe it.

Where do the figures come from?

Post counts, follower counts and available engagement figures were collected from public sources on 13 October 2026. Ratios and index scores are calculated from those observations. Ad counts are search minimums from the Meta Ad Library on the same day, grouped by the advertiser page that placed them, and unavailable values remain blank. The post log includes source links.
