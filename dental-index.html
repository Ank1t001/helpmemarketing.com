Dental Implant Marketing Index

How five selected Ontario dental clinics in the Greater Toronto Area present and promote implant consultations, what an owner can compare against their own clinic, and three tests to discuss with a marketing team. A study of published marketing, not a ranking of clinics or a judgement of clinical care.

The short answer

Draft. Of the five selected clinics, I could read three websites so far: Studio B Dental, North York Smile Centre and Dr. Neil J. Gajjar Associates & Specialists. Two of the three publish implant prices before the enquiry, and all three ask visitors to send a request and wait to be contacted rather than book a confirmed time. Dundas East Dental and Bond Street Dental, their Instagram posts and all advertising checks are still to be verified, so this page is not a finished comparison.

What stood out

Findings from the published implant pages, consultation offers and enquiry forms of the three clinics whose websites I could read. Each names its examples and the limitation needed to read it; none claims that a feature caused better results.

1. Two of the three readable clinics publish implant prices before the enquiry

Studio B Dental and North York Smile Centre both give figures for a single implant and for full-arch treatment. North York Smile Centre sets out three full-arch levels per arch, each tied to a restoration material and shown with a monthly figure on approved credit. Studio B Dental gives a starting fee on its All-on-X page and a different full-set range on another implant page. Dr. Gajjar's implant page gives no figure and closes with a call to schedule a consultation.

Limitation. Published starting prices are the clinics' own statements with different inclusions, so they are not comparable with each other, and two of the five clinics could not be read yet.

Sources: smile-by-design.ca/procedures/all-on-x-dental-implants, smile-by-design.ca/procedures/dental-implants, studiobdental.ca/full-mouth-restoration, studiobdental.ca/toronto-dental-implants, drgajjar.com/dental-implants-all-on-x

What this means for your clinic

Cost is one of the first questions an implant patient has, and two of these clinics answer it before asking for contact details. Ask your team what a visitor to your implant page learns about cost before they have to call, and which cost questions your front desk hears every week.

2. The consultation offer is described in very different detail

Studio B Dental lists five appointment options with their length and fee, from a complimentary screening or second opinion to a paid two-visit exam, and its All-on-X page asks for a free screening and estimate. North York Smile Centre offers a free consultation and describes it as no-obligation. Dr. Gajjar's implant page asks visitors to call to schedule a consultation without saying what it includes or whether it has a fee.

Limitation. These are published offers. Whether staff describe them the same way on the phone, and how many people choose each option, cannot be seen from outside.

Sources: studiobdental.ca/dental-implants-toronto, studiobdental.ca/full-mouth-restoration, smile-by-design.ca home page, smile-by-design.ca/procedures/all-on-x-dental-implants, drgajjar.com/dental-implants-all-on-x

What this means for your clinic

A patient deciding whether to enquire is also judging what the first visit will cost in time and money. Ask your team to write down what your implant consultation includes and costs, then check that your implant page and your phone script say the same thing.

3. Every enquiry route I could read ends in a request, not a confirmed booking

All three readable clinics ask the visitor to send details and wait to be contacted. Studio B Dental uses a virtual consult form that offers smile photo uploads; North York Smile Centre and Dr. Gajjar use appointment request forms that ask for a preferred date and time. North York Smile Centre explains the next steps on its home page, Studio B Dental says a team member will be in touch, and Dr. Gajjar's appointment request page does not say what happens next. None of the forms reviewed asks an implant question, such as how many teeth are missing or whether the person wears a denture.

Limitation. No form was submitted, so response times and follow-up are unknown. A stated promise is not a tested result.

Sources: studiobdental.ca home page, studiobdental.ca/dental-implants-toronto, smile-by-design.ca/procedures/all-on-x-dental-implants, smile-by-design.ca home page, drgajjar.com/appointment-request

What this means for your clinic

Between the form and the first call, an implant enquiry can go cold, and a generic form tells your team little about the case before they call. Ask your team how long recent implant enquiries waited for a first reply, and whether your form tells people what happens next.

Clinic by clinic

For each clinic: its implant positioning, what its website shows, what that evidence supports, what remains unknown, and what it means for your clinic. Observations come from the pages; interpretations are mine and are labelled as such. None of these clinics is an HMM client.

Studio B Dental (@studiobdental)

Positioning. Full-arch replacement for people with failing teeth or dentures they dislike, under a same-day brand, Teeth-In-1-Day, with separate pages for single implants and metal-free zirconia implants.

Observed marketing. A starting fee on the All-on-X page with the two treatment phases spelled out, a price range on a second implant page, five consultation options with lengths and fees, a site-wide virtual consult form with optional smile photo uploads, an implant guide covering bone, pain and insurance, and the lead dentist named and described as teaching the procedure.

What the evidence supports. The website is built around one clinician and one full-arch offer, and it answers cost and consultation questions before asking for contact. My reading: the two cost figures, on different pages and described in different ways, could leave a visitor unsure which applies. That is a reading of the pages, not a measured effect.

What remains unknown. Instagram posts were not collected, Meta and Google ads were not checked, the image galleries were not reviewed and response to the form was not tested.

Sources: studiobdental.ca/full-mouth-restoration, studiobdental.ca/toronto-dental-implants, studiobdental.ca/dental-implants-toronto, studiobdental.ca home page, studiobdental.ca/need-to-know-dental-implants, studiobdental.ca/zirconia-dental-implants

What this means for your clinic

When cost appears on more than one page, every figure needs to describe the same thing. Ask your team to list each implant price or range on your site and check that each one says what it includes.

North York Smile Centre (@northyork.smilecentre)

Positioning. A family practice that gives full-arch implants their own page, opening with situations such as loose dentures and teeth that cannot be saved, alongside single-tooth implants.

Observed marketing. A single-implant range with what it includes, three All-on-X levels per arch tied to restoration materials and shown with monthly figures on approved credit, a named financing partner, a free, no-obligation consultation, a six-step treatment journey, the planning technology explained, an appointment request form with preferred date and time, and a three-step explanation of what happens after a request.

What the evidence supports. Of the three websites I could read, this one sets out the fullest published picture of price and process: levels, materials, monthly figures and the steps from consultation to final teeth are all visible before the enquiry. Whether that changes who enquires is a hypothesis, not something public pages can show.

What remains unknown. Instagram posts were not collected, Meta and Google ads were not checked, and the reviews shown mostly describe general and surgical visits rather than implants.

Sources: smile-by-design.ca/procedures/all-on-x-dental-implants, smile-by-design.ca/procedures/dental-implants, smile-by-design.ca/financing, smile-by-design.ca home page

What this means for your clinic

Tiered pricing turns one large number into a choice between levels. If you publish full-arch fees, test showing levels by material and measure which consultations go ahead and at what level, from your own records.

Dr. Neil J. Gajjar Associates & Specialists (@gajjardentistry)

Positioning. A multi-specialty practice in Mississauga that covers single implants and All-on-4 or All-on-X on one page, framed around missing teeth and an evaluation of the right option.

Observed marketing. Six questions and answers on candidacy, process, full-arch options, lifespan and bone loss, clinician profiles including a prosthodontist, no price or financing figure on the implant page, a call to schedule a consultation, and an appointment request form that requires a date, time, patient status and contact details.

What the evidence supports. The page leads with clinician credentials and plain answers rather than price or an offer. Because single-tooth and full-arch treatment share one page, its opening speaks to both situations at once.

What remains unknown. Instagram posts were not collected, Meta and Google ads were not checked, the smile gallery's implant cases could not be confirmed and response to the form was not tested.

Sources: drgajjar.com/dental-implants-all-on-x, drgajjar.com/appointment-request, drgajjar.com/smile-gallery

What this means for your clinic

Credentials answer who will treat the patient, but not what it will cost or what happens next. Ask your team which questions implant callers ask most, and whether your implant page answers them before the form.

Across the clinics

The same dimensions compared across the clinics I could read. These are observations from three selected clinics, not patterns in Ontario dentistry.

Single-tooth and full-arch patients are addressed on separate pages at two clinics, on one page at the third

Studio B Dental and North York Smile Centre each give full-arch treatment its own page that opens with a full-arch situation, such as failing teeth or loose dentures, and keep single implants on another page. Dr. Gajjar covers both on one page under a missing-teeth heading. These are different patient situations with different costs and decisions, so a shared page has to speak to both at once.

Sources: studiobdental.ca/full-mouth-restoration, studiobdental.ca/toronto-dental-implants, smile-by-design.ca/procedures/all-on-x-dental-implants, smile-by-design.ca/procedures/dental-implants, drgajjar.com/dental-implants-all-on-x

What this means for your clinic

Someone searching for full-arch treatment and someone missing one tooth need different first answers. Ask your team where each implant ad, post and email link points, and whether full-arch traffic lands on a full-arch page.

Clinician-led proof at two clinics, process and technology at the third

Studio B Dental builds its implant pages around its lead dentist, whom it describes as performing and teaching the procedure. Dr. Gajjar's implant page carries the clinicians' credentials, including a prosthodontist. North York Smile Centre leads instead with its planning technology and a step-by-step treatment journey. All three describe their own experience in their own words; I have not verified those statements.

Sources: studiobdental.ca/full-mouth-restoration, studiobdental.ca/contact-us, drgajjar.com/dental-implants-all-on-x, smile-by-design.ca/procedures/all-on-x-dental-implants, smile-by-design.ca/procedures/dental-implants

What this means for your clinic

Patients weighing a large treatment want to know both who will do it and what will happen. As a hypothesis to test, swap the first section of your implant page between a clinician introduction and a step-by-step journey for a few weeks each, and measure qualified implant enquiries per page visit from your own form and call records.

Outcome and experience claims appear without sources

Studio B Dental states a success rate and a five-year continuing care warranty; North York Smile Centre states that its team has placed thousands of implants and shows patient reviews that mostly describe other treatment. None of these statements links to a source or set of terms on the page reviewed.

Sources: studiobdental.ca/toronto-dental-implants, studiobdental.ca/need-to-know-dental-implants, smile-by-design.ca/procedures/dental-implants, smile-by-design.ca/financing

What this means for your clinic

Claims carry more weight when a patient can see where they come from. Ask your team which claims on your implant pages you could document if a patient asked for the source or the terms.

Educational answers come before the contact prompt at all three

Each readable clinic answers common implant questions on or near its implant pages before asking for contact: Studio B Dental in an implant guide covering bone, pain and insurance, North York Smile Centre in an implant FAQ, and Dr. Gajjar in a page written entirely as questions and answers. Whether these pages appear in search results or AI answers was not checked.

Sources: studiobdental.ca/need-to-know-dental-implants, smile-by-design.ca/procedures/dental-implants, drgajjar.com/dental-implants-all-on-x

What this means for your clinic

An answer is most useful when the next step sits right after it. Ask your team whether each of your implant answers ends with the matching next step, such as a cost page, the consultation details or the form.

Three practical tests

Each test comes from a finding above and is measured with your own records. There are no baselines, targets or expected lifts here: what counts as a change depends on your own numbers. A before-and-after comparison is not a controlled split test, and lead outcomes need equal follow-up time.

Test 1. Show what full-arch treatment costs before the form (finding 1)

Evidence that prompted it
Two of the three readable clinics publish implant prices before the enquiry; one sets out full-arch levels by material with monthly figures, and one leads with a consultation instead.
Hypothesis
A clear cost block on the full-arch page, with starting prices and what each includes, may change who enquires: fewer enquiries from people who would stop at the price, and more who go on to a consultation.
The one change
Add one cost block to your full-arch page: your starting prices, what each includes and your financing route.
What stays the same
Ads, budgets, targeting, the rest of the page, the form and how quickly your team replies.
Primary measure
Share of full-arch enquiries that reach a completed consultation, from your own records.
Guardrails
Total full-arch enquiries, and cost per completed consultation if you run ads. Prices must be accurate and match what the front desk quotes.
How and when to compare
Compare a fixed period before and after the change, each given the same follow-up time so slow-moving enquiries can complete. This is a before-and-after comparison, not a controlled split test, so note anything else that changed, such as season or ad spend.
Not enough evidence if
Too few full-arch enquiries in either period to tell a difference from normal variation, or a large change in ads or season during the test. Extend the test rather than draw a conclusion.

Test 2. Say what the implant consultation includes (finding 2)

Evidence that prompted it
One readable clinic lists its consultation options with length and fee, one offers a free no-obligation consultation, and one asks visitors to call without saying what the consultation includes.
Hypothesis
Stating what the first visit includes, how long it takes and whether it costs anything may increase the share of enquiries that book and attend a consultation.
The one change
Add a short consultation description beside the main call to action on your implant page.
What stays the same
The consultation itself, its price, the form, ads and reply times.
Primary measure
Share of implant enquiries that book and then attend a consultation.
Guardrails
No-show rate, and staff time spent explaining the consultation on the phone.
How and when to compare
Compare equal periods before and after, counting only enquiries old enough to have had the same time to book and attend. If your website tool can split visitors between two versions at random, that is stronger than a before-and-after comparison.
Not enough evidence if
Too few implant enquiries to separate the change from normal week-to-week variation, or a change to the consultation offer itself during the test.

Test 3. Ask one implant question on the enquiry form (finding 3)

Evidence that prompted it
None of the forms reviewed asks an implant question; all three readable clinics rely on general contact and appointment fields, and one adds optional smile photos.
Hypothesis
One short multiple-choice question, such as one missing tooth, several, all teeth in one jaw, or a denture, may let your team prepare a better first call and raise the share of implant enquiries that reach a consultation.
The one change
Add one optional multiple-choice question about the patient's situation to the implant enquiry form.
What stays the same
Every other form field, page content, ads and reply times. Keep the question optional so it does not block an enquiry.
Primary measure
Share of implant enquiries that reach a completed consultation, and the time from enquiry to first contact, from your own records.
Guardrails
Form completion rate and total enquiries. Do not ask for clinical photos or health details in the form without the right privacy handling.
How and when to compare
Compare equal periods before and after the change, with equal follow-up time for each enquiry. Treat it as a before-and-after comparison, not a controlled split test.
Not enough evidence if
Too few implant enquiries to compare, a fall in form completions that offsets any gain, or answers that are mostly left blank.

Method, sources and limitations

How the evidence was collected, what was left out and why. This research assesses published marketing, not clinical quality, treatment outcomes or patient suitability.

What is not measured, and how to read the evidence

Nothing on this page estimates advertising budgets, revenue, return on ad spend, cost per lead, consultation conversion, treatment acceptance, agency fees or appointment capacity; public pages cannot show them. A website credit to a marketing provider shows a published association, not a current contract, its value or its scope. A tracking script, pixel or separate phone number does not show that automation, retargeting or attribution works, and a stated response promise is not a measured response time. No form was submitted, no appointment booked, no photo uploaded and no clinic contacted.

Each statement is labelled as an observation (what a page shows), a clinic claim (what a clinic says about itself, reported, not endorsed) or my interpretation.
