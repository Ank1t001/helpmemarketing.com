Dental Implant Marketing Index

How five selected Ontario dental clinics in the Greater Toronto Area present and promote implant consultations, what an owner can compare against their own clinic, and three tests to discuss with a marketing team. A study of published marketing, not a ranking of clinics or a judgement of clinical care.

The short answer

Draft. Of the five selected clinics, I could read three websites: Studio B Dental, North York Smile Centre and Dr. Neil J. Gajjar Associates & Specialists. Two of the three publish implant prices before the enquiry, and all three ask visitors to send a request and wait to be contacted rather than book a confirmed time. In the ad library searches for these three clinics, the one implant ad found was an All-on-X offer from a Meta page that shares its name with Studio B Dental's Instagram account; it was running when I checked, the day after the window ended, and its price is worded differently from the clinic's website. The ads found for North York Smile Centre were for Invisalign, although most of its observed posts were about full-arch implants, and those for Dr. Gajjar promoted general dental care. Dundas East Dental's and Bond Street Dental's websites could not be read and the Instagram evidence is an incomplete sample, so this page is not a finished comparison.

What stood out

Findings from the published implant pages, consultation offers and enquiry forms of the three clinics whose websites I could read, with the Instagram posts and ad library records observed for the same clinics. Each names its examples and the limitation needed to read it; none claims that a feature caused better results.

1. Two of the three readable clinics publish implant prices before the enquiry

Studio B Dental and North York Smile Centre both give figures for a single implant and for full-arch treatment. North York Smile Centre sets out three full-arch levels per arch, each tied to a restoration material and shown with a monthly figure on approved credit. Studio B Dental gives a starting fee on its All-on-X page and a different full-set range on another implant page. Dr. Gajjar's implant page gives no figure and closes with a call to schedule a consultation. An All-on-X ad in Meta Ad Library from the page "studiobdental", which shares its name with the clinic's Instagram account and was running when I checked, gives a per-arch figure and prices a final bridge separately, while the website gives a different starting fee and, in the text I saved, no separate fee for the final bridge. The two do not describe their inclusions in the same way, so the difference is unresolved.

Limitation. Published starting prices are the clinics' own statements with different inclusions, so they are not comparable with each other. The ad's link to the clinic rests on its page name, which supports but does not confirm it. I saw the ad and the page on the same day, which does not show which figure a patient is quoted, and two of the five clinics could not be read.

Sources: smile-by-design.ca/procedures/all-on-x-dental-implants, smile-by-design.ca/procedures/dental-implants, studiobdental.ca/full-mouth-restoration, studiobdental.ca/toronto-dental-implants, drgajjar.com/dental-implants-all-on-x, Meta Ad Library record 26002231369395543

What this means for your clinic

Cost is one of the first questions an implant patient has, and two of these clinics answer it before asking for contact details. Check that every price in your ads matches the page or message it leads to, with the same inclusions, and ask your team which cost questions the front desk hears every week.

2. The consultation offer is described in very different detail

Studio B Dental lists five appointment options with their length and fee, from a complimentary screening or second opinion to a paid two-visit exam, and its All-on-X page asks for a free screening and estimate. North York Smile Centre offers a free consultation and describes it as no-obligation. Dr. Gajjar's implant page asks visitors to call to schedule a consultation without saying what it includes or whether it has a fee.

Limitation. These are published offers. Whether staff describe them the same way on the phone, and how many people choose each option, cannot be seen from outside.

Sources: studiobdental.ca/dental-implants-toronto, studiobdental.ca/full-mouth-restoration, smile-by-design.ca home page, smile-by-design.ca/procedures/all-on-x-dental-implants, drgajjar.com/dental-implants-all-on-x

What this means for your clinic

A patient deciding whether to enquire is also judging what the first visit will cost in time and money. Ask your team to write down what your implant consultation includes and costs, then check that your implant page and your phone script say the same thing.

3. Every enquiry route I could read ends in a request, not a confirmed booking

All three readable clinics ask the visitor to send details and wait to be contacted. Studio B Dental uses a virtual consult form that offers smile photo uploads; North York Smile Centre and Dr. Gajjar use appointment request forms that ask for a preferred date and time. North York Smile Centre explains the next steps on its home page, Studio B Dental says a team member will be in touch, and Dr. Gajjar's appointment request page does not say what happens next. None of the forms reviewed asks an implant question, such as how many teeth are missing or whether the person wears a denture.

Limitation. No form was submitted, so response times and follow-up are unknown. A stated promise is not a tested result.

Sources: studiobdental.ca home page, studiobdental.ca/dental-implants-toronto, smile-by-design.ca/procedures/all-on-x-dental-implants, smile-by-design.ca home page, drgajjar.com/appointment-request

What this means for your clinic

Between the form and the first call, an implant enquiry can go cold, and a generic form tells your team little about the case before they call. Ask your team how long recent implant enquiries waited for a first reply, and whether your form tells people what happens next.

4. The ad library searches for the three readable clinics found one implant ad

I searched Meta Ad Library by exact name and Google Ads Transparency by domain for the three clinics whose websites I could read. The one implant ad found was an All-on-X offer from the Meta page "studiobdental", which shares its name with the Instagram account Studio B Dental's website links; that supports, but does not confirm, its link to the clinic. It was running when I checked, the day after the window ended, and its library start date is 21 January 2026; the library does not show whether it ran during the window. The other ads found promote other treatments: veneers from the same page, an Invisalign offer from the page North York Smile Centre with start dates between 15 September and 5 October, and general dental care from Dr. Gajjar's page, which links to the clinic's website, started on 12 October. A veneers ad from the head-office page My Smile Centre is kept apart because its link to the North York clinic is uncertain. Google showed no ads for these three domains in the window; North York Smile Centre's implant ads there were last shown before it, in November 2025. On Instagram, three of the five posts observed from North York Smile Centre were about full-arch implants.

Limitation. These were exact-name and domain searches run once, the day after the window ended, so they do not describe all of a clinic's advertising: an ad under another page name, or pointing to another site, would not appear. Meta shows a start date and whether an ad was running when checked, not day-by-day delivery, so an ad that started before the window and was running afterwards is not shown to have run inside it. Dundas East Dental and Bond Street Dental are left out because their websites could not be read, and nothing here shows budget or results.

What this means for your clinic

What a clinic posts about and what it pays to promote are separate decisions. Ask your team which treatment each of your live ads promotes today, and whether that matches the treatment you most want enquiries for.

Studio B Dental (@studiobdental)

Positioning. Full-arch replacement for people with failing teeth or dentures they dislike, under a same-day brand, Teeth-In-1-Day, with separate pages for single implants and metal-free zirconia implants.

Observed marketing. A starting fee on the All-on-X page with the two treatment phases spelled out, a price range on a second implant page, five consultation options with lengths and fees, a site-wide virtual consult form with optional smile photo uploads, an implant guide covering bone, pain and insurance, and the lead dentist named and described as teaching the procedure. Four posts were observed on its Instagram account in the window: one implant testimonial, one whose hashtags mention implant surgery and veneers, and two about cosmetic work and clinic life. Meta Ad Library showed two ads from the page "studiobdental", whose name matches the clinic's Instagram account, which supports but does not confirm the link: an All-on-X offer and a veneers offer. Both were running when I checked and both have library start dates before the window, so the library does not show whether they ran during it. Google Ads Transparency showed no ads for its domain in the documented search.

What the evidence supports. The website is built around one clinician and one full-arch offer, and it answers cost and consultation questions before asking for contact. My reading: the cost figures on different pages, and a different figure in the All-on-X ad, could leave a visitor unsure which applies. That is a reading of the published offers, not a measured effect.

What remains unknown. Whether the account published other posts in the window beyond the four observed, play counts, whether the All-on-X ad ran at any point during the window, which fee a patient is quoted, the image galleries, and how the form is answered.

What this means for your clinic

When cost appears in more than one place, every figure needs to describe the same thing. Ask your team to list each implant price in your ads and on your site with what it includes, and check that they agree.

North York Smile Centre (@northyork.smilecentre)

Positioning. A family practice that gives full-arch implants their own page, opening with situations such as loose dentures and teeth that cannot be saved, alongside single-tooth implants.

Observed marketing. A single-implant range with what it includes, three All-on-X levels per arch tied to restoration materials and shown with monthly figures on approved credit, a named financing partner, a free, no-obligation consultation, a six-step treatment journey, the planning technology explained, an appointment request form with preferred date and time, and a three-step explanation of what happens after a request. Five posts were observed on its Instagram account in the window: three about full-arch implants (a patient testimonial, an All-on-X question reel and a webinar invitation), one about emergencies and one about Invisalign. The Meta ads found under its name were one Invisalign offer, running when I checked, with start dates between 15 September and 5 October; a veneers ad from the head-office page My Smile Centre is kept apart because its link to this clinic is uncertain. Its implant ads in Google Ads Transparency, placed by OneCore Media Corp., were last shown before the window, in November 2025.

What the evidence supports. Of the three websites I could read, this one sets out the fullest published picture of price and process: levels, materials, monthly figures and the steps from consultation to final teeth are all visible before the enquiry. Whether that changes who enquires is a hypothesis, not something public pages can show. Three practical tests

Each test comes from a finding above and is measured with your own records. There are no baselines, targets or expected lifts here: what counts as a change depends on your own numbers. A before-and-after comparison is not a controlled split test, and lead outcomes need equal follow-up time.

Test 1. Show what full-arch treatment costs before the form (finding 1)

Evidence that prompted it
Two of the three readable clinics publish implant prices before the enquiry; one sets out full-arch levels by material with monthly figures, one leads with a consultation instead, and one clinic's ad and website describe its full-arch fee differently.
Hypothesis
A clear cost block on the full-arch page, with starting prices and what each includes, may change who enquires: fewer enquiries from people who would stop at the price, and more who go on to a consultation.
The one change
Add one cost block to your full-arch page: your starting prices, what each includes and your financing route.
What stays the same
Ads, budgets, targeting, the rest of the page, the form and how quickly your team replies.
Primary measure
Share of full-arch enquiries that reach a completed consultation, from your own records.
Guardrails
Total full-arch enquiries, and cost per completed consultation if you run ads. Prices must be accurate and match what the front desk quotes and what your ads say.
How and when to compare
Compare a fixed period before and after the change, each given the same follow-up time so slow-moving enquiries can complete. This is a before-and-after comparison, not a controlled split test, so note anything else that changed, such as season or ad spend.
Not enough evidence if
Too few full-arch enquiries in either period to tell a difference from normal variation, or a large change in ads or season during the test. Extend the test rather than draw a conclusion.

Test 2. Say what the implant consultation includes (finding 2)

Evidence that prompted it
One readable clinic lists its consultation options with length and fee, one offers a free no-obligation consultation, and one asks visitors to call without saying what the consultation includes.
Hypothesis
Stating what the first visit includes, how long it takes and whether it costs anything may increase the share of enquiries that book and attend a consultation.
The one change
Add a short consultation description beside the main call to action on your implant page.
What stays the same
The consultation itself, its price, the form, ads and reply times.
Primary measure
Share of implant enquiries that book and then attend a consultation.
Guardrails
No-show rate, and staff time spent explaining the consultation on the phone.
How and when to compare
Compare equal periods before and after, counting only enquiries old enough to have had the same time to book and attend. If your website tool can split visitors between two versions at random, that is stronger than a before-and-after comparison.
Not enough evidence if
Too few implant enquiries to separate the change from normal week-to-week variation, or a change to the consultation offer itself during the test.

Test 3. Ask one implant question on the enquiry form (finding 3)

Evidence that prompted it
None of the forms reviewed asks an implant question; all three readable clinics rely on general contact and appointment fields, and one adds optional smile photos.
Hypothesis
One short multiple-choice question, such as one missing tooth, several, all teeth in one jaw, or a denture, may let your team prepare a better first call and raise the share of implant enquiries that reach a consultation.
The one change
Add one optional multiple-choice question about the patient's situation to the implant enquiry form.
What stays the same
Every other form field, page content, ads and reply times. Keep the question optional so it does not block an enquiry.
Primary measure
Share of implant enquiries that reach a completed consultation, and the time from enquiry to first contact, from your own records.
Guardrails
Form completion rate and total enquiries. Do not ask for clinical photos or health details in the form without the right privacy handling.
How and when to compare
Compare equal periods before and after the change, with equal follow-up time for each enquiry. Treat it as a before-and-after comparison, not a controlled split test.
Not enough evidence if
Too few implant enquiries to compare, a fall in form completions that offsets any gain, or answers that are mostly left blank.
