You sometimes need two pages to understand the reward

2 of 5 platforms that published a reward rate

Venn and Loop published reward statements that did not agree for some plans. A business owner needs the rate and its conditions together to understand which spending qualifies.

Evidence for this finding

Reward rates and their conditions were often stated in different places

What the sources say
Five of the six platforms published a reward rate. At two, visible statements on different pages did not agree for some plans: Venn's cards page says its cashback has no tiers, thresholds or caps, while its pricing footnote says cashback is earned only on the first $5,000 of card spend a month on Essentials and the first $25,000 on Plus; Loop's cards page says rewards come on all spend, while its earn-rate table lists USD, EUR and GBP spend as not eligible on the Basic plan. Airwallex's visible pages say 1%; a 2% figure appears only in the page's title metadata. Float and Keep publish their conditions on the pricing page. Denominator: 5 platforms that published a reward rate. For Ramp, a Canadian rewards rate was not found in the reviewed sources. Evidence: Venn, rewards; Loop, rewards; Airwallex, rewards; Float, rewards; Keep, rewards; Ramp, rewards
Customer question
“Is the advertised reward what I would earn?”
My reading
A reader who meets the rate on one page and its condition on another has to connect the two to know what they would earn.

“No FX fee” doesn’t cover every transaction

6 of 6 platforms limited the no-FX-fee claim to a specific situation

All six platforms used a no-FX-fee claim for a specific situation. What you pay can depend on the currency, the balance you hold and the transaction.

Compare FX conditions →

Evidence for this finding

Every "no FX fee" claim described one kind of transaction

What the sources say
All six platforms published a statement of no FX fee, no FX markup or a 0% FX fee. Each covered a specific case, such as spending from a balance in the same currency, four approved currencies or a USD card, and each site or its terms describes a fee, rate or margin for other conversions: for example an automatic conversion fee in Airwallex's card terms, and a note on Ramp's global page that conversions stay subject to exchange rates and fees. Keep's pricing page lists an FX fee of 0% for banking, while its card agreement sets a 3% card conversion fee. Denominator: 6 of 6 platforms; card spending, FX conversion and transfers were checked separately for each. Evidence: Venn, card spending in another currency; Loop, card spending in another currency; Keep, fx conversion; Airwallex, card spending in another currency; Float, card spending in another currency; Ramp, card spending in another currency
Customer question
“Does "no FX fee" mean I never pay to convert?”
My reading
A general "no FX fee" line answers the question only for the case it covers; the other cases are priced on other pages or in the terms.
Analysis and test
Currency fees in Compare products. Proposed test, not a result: test 2, Explain what “no FX fee” covers.

Most first screens didn’t explain what happens next

1 of 6 first screens listed the steps ahead

Only Loop’s first screen listed the steps ahead. The six routes served different purposes, including account sign-ups, a card application and a demo request. Keep’s self-serve application route was closed when checked.

Evidence for this finding

Few first screens said who can apply or what happens next

What the sources say
I followed each platform's main start button to its first screen in a browser. Loop's sign-up was the only one that listed the steps ahead and what they need (CRA ID, date of birth, government ID). Ramp states its eligibility rules (entity, Business Number, Canadian bank account) on its card page, but its sign-up screen asks only for an email. Venn's sign-up says there is no personal credit check, while its Quebec exclusion appears elsewhere on the site. Float's demo form and Airwallex's sign-up ask for contact details without saying what follows, and Keep's start buttons lead to a closed-applications page. Denominator: 6 of 6 first screens observed in a browser on 23 October 2026; none was followed past the first screen. “Can I tell whether I qualify, and what happens after I start?”

At most first screens, whether you qualify and what follows was on another page or not stated before the next step. This is a reading of six first screens, not of how many people start or finish an application.

About this study

This page serves two kinds of reader. If you are comparing card and spend platforms, it sets out what six providers publish, each fact linked to its source. If you market a product, it is my independent case study of how they explain it: the offer, the proof and the path to applying. None of the six is a client, and I have no view of their internal strategy or results.

What is compared
What six providers publish about their business cards, cross-border payments and spend tools, and how their pages and first application screens explain them.
Why these six
I selected them as a comparison set: each markets business cards or spend management to Canadian businesses. It is a selected cohort, not the six largest or best, and not a complete survey of the market.
The Canadian context
Product facts come from Canadian pages or terms that apply to Canadian businesses. Three of the six state Quebec exclusions (Venn for all Quebec businesses, Loop and Airwallex for Quebec sole proprietors). Ramp's customer count is worldwide, and Airwallex's figures do not say whether they are global or Canadian; both are labelled that way.
What it can and cannot establish
It can establish what a public page said at a recorded time, and where two statements differ. It cannot establish how the products perform, what customers understand or do, how well any provider's marketing works, why a provider wrote what it did, or whether anything complies with regulation.

Sources and methodology

Scope

An independent comparative marketing case study of how six selected platforms explain business cards, spend management and cross-border payments to Canadian businesses. It reads dated public sources and the first screen of each application journey. It does not test product performance, assess regulatory compliance or recommend a provider for your business.

Selection

The founder selected these six platforms as a comparison set of providers that market business cards and spend management to Canadian businesses. They are not presented as the largest, the most used or the best, and other providers exist.

  • US and global pages were not used as Canadian facts. Where a Canadian page carried US disclosures, that is recorded as an observation about the page.
  • Regulatory statements and registry entries (FINTRAC and the Bank of Canada, checked on 23 October 2026) are recorded in each platform's evidence as supporting detail. They are not a compliance assessment, an endorsement or a safety rating.
  • This edition does not compare FX costs or product performance and does not recommend a provider.

Lessons & tests

Three changes to test on your own website. These are proposals, not proven results.

  1. Proposed test, not a result

    Test 1. Put the condition beside the reward

    The change
    Show the spending threshold, cap or plan restriction beside the reward rate.
    What to measure
    Qualified applications per page visitor, alongside reward-related questions.
    Evidence and test setup

    From finding 1.

    Observed evidence
    Venn's cards page says its cashback has no tiers, thresholds or caps, while its pricing footnote limits the eligible card spend on Essentials and Plus. Loop's cards page says rewards come on all spend, while its earn-rate table lists USD, EUR and GBP spend as not eligible on Basic. (O-VEN-04, O-LOP-04)
    Customer question
    Will I earn the advertised rate on my plan and at my spend level?
    My interpretation
    When the rate and its condition sit on different pages, a reader of either page sees only part of the offer.
    Testable hypothesis
    With the condition beside the rate, readers can check it before applying, so the version with the condition will produce at least as many qualified applications per card-page visitor, with fewer rewards questions from new customers.
    One change
    Show the cap, threshold or plan condition in the same block as the headline rate on the card page.
    What stays comparable
    The rate, the terms, the design and the button stay the same. Split card-page traffic at random, with the same traffic sources, the same attribution window and the same definition of a qualified application in both versions.
    Primary measurement
    Qualified applications (meeting your eligibility rules) per visitor to the card page. Count button clicks separately.
    Guardrail
    Rewards questions to sales and support in the first 30 days after sign-up, and plan mix.
    When the result would be inconclusive
    Inconclusive if rewards are not a main reason buyers choose you, if traffic is too small to separate the two versions, or if pricing or rewards change during the test. Legal review of the wording comes first.
  2. Proposed test, not a result

    Test 2. Explain what “no FX fee” covers

    The change
    Name the transaction covered and explain what applies otherwise.
    What to measure
    Completed applications per page visitor, alongside FX questions and disputed charges.
    Evidence and test setup

    From finding 2.

    Observed evidence
    All six platforms published a no-FX-fee statement that covered a specific case, such as a matching-currency balance, four currencies or a USD card, and each site or its terms prices other conversions elsewhere. Keep's pricing page lists an FX fee of 0% for banking while its card agreement sets a 3% card conversion fee. (O-VEN-08, O-LOP-07, O-KEP-08, O-AWX-07, O-FLT-08, O-RMP-07)
    Customer question
    Will I pay to convert when I buy in a currency I do not hold?
    My interpretation
    A general line answers that question only for the case it covers; the reader has to find the other cases in pricing or the terms.
    Testable hypothesis
    A line that names the covered transaction, followed by one line on what applies otherwise, will produce at least as many completed applications from the FX or cards page, with fewer FX questions and disputed charges after sign-up.
    One change
    Replace a general "no FX fees" line with one that names the case it covers, followed by one line saying what applies otherwise.
    What stays comparable
    The page layout, offer, fees, terms and button stay the same. Split traffic at random with the same audiences, the same visitor definition and the same attribution window. If traffic is too low to split, matched consecutive periods give a directional reading only: timing, traffic mix and other changes can affect the result, so they are not equivalent to random assignment. Record the dates.
    Primary measurement
    Completed applications per visitor to the FX or cards page. Report the application count and the visitor count as well as the rate, with the same visitor definition and attribution window in both versions.
    Guardrail
    FX-related support questions and disputed charges in the first 60 days after sign-up.
    When the result would be inconclusive
    Inconclusive if seasonal travel or trade cycles swamp the difference, or if fees change during the test. Required disclosures stay in place; this changes the summary, not the terms.
  3. Proposed test, not a result

    Test 3. Explain the next step before the form

    The change
    State who can apply, what they need and what happens after submitting.
    What to measure
    Qualified applications per visitor and per form start. Track total qualified applications too.
    Evidence and test setup

    From finding 3.

    Observed evidence
    Loop's sign-up lists the two steps ahead and what they need. Ramp's eligibility rules sit on its cards page rather than its sign-up screen. The Venn, Float and Airwallex first screens asked for contact or account details without saying what follows, and Keep's route was closed. (O-LOP-15, O-RMP-04, O-RMP-15, O-VEN-18, O-FLT-16, O-AWX-14)
    Customer question
    Do I qualify, and what happens after I give my details?
    My interpretation
    At most first screens the answer to either question was on another page or not stated before the next step.
    Testable hypothesis
    A short "Before you start" list above the first field will raise the share of form starts that become qualified applications, without reducing the number of qualified applications.
    One change
    Add a short "Before you start" list above the first form field: who can apply, what you need, which provinces are served, and what happens after submitting.
    What stays comparable
    The form fields, verification steps, traffic sources and the definition of a qualified application stay the same. Split traffic at random with consistent audiences.
    Primary measurement
    Qualified applications per visitor and per form start, and total qualified applications. Report clicks, form starts, submissions and qualified applications as separate numbers. A higher qualification rate alone is not enough: if total qualified applications fall, the list is turning away businesses that would have qualified.
    Guardrail
    Eligible visitors who start the form, and total qualified applications.
    When the result would be inconclusive
    Inconclusive if eligibility rules change during the test or most traffic is already qualified. Never remove necessary identity or eligibility checks.

Run these tests using your own website and application data. This research uses public evidence only; measuring a result needs your own traffic and application records, and no baselines, sample sizes or uplift have been established here.
