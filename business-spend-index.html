My reading of the evidence
Is the advertised reward what I would earn?

Reward rates and their conditions were often stated in different places

Five of the six platforms published a reward rate. At two, visible statements on different pages did not agree for some plans: Venn's cards page says its cashback has no tiers, thresholds or caps, while its pricing footnote says cashback is earned only on the first $5,000 of card spend a month on Essentials and the first $25,000 on Plus; Loop's cards page says rewards come on all spend, while its earn-rate table lists USD, EUR and GBP spend as not eligible on the Basic plan. Airwallex's visible pages say 1%; a 2% figure appears only in the page's title metadata. Float and Keep publish their conditions on the pricing page. Denominator: 5 platforms that published a reward rate. For Ramp, a Canadian rewards rate was not found in the reviewed sources.

Evidence: Venn, rewards; Loop, rewards; Airwallex, rewards; Float, rewards; Keep, rewards; Ramp, rewards

Does "no FX fee" mean I never pay to convert?

Every "no FX fee" claim described one kind of transaction

All six platforms published a statement of no FX fee, no FX markup or a 0% FX fee. Each covered a specific case, such as spending from a balance in the same currency, four approved currencies or a USD card, and each site or its terms describes a fee, rate or margin for other conversions: for example an automatic conversion fee in Airwallex's card terms, and a note on Ramp's global page that conversions stay subject to exchange rates and fees. Keep's pricing page lists an FX fee of 0% for banking, while its card agreement sets a 3% card conversion fee. Denominator: 6 of 6 platforms; card spending, FX conversion and transfers were checked separately for each.

Evidence: Venn, card spending in another currency; Loop, card spending in another currency; Keep, fx conversion; Airwallex, card spending in another currency; Float, card spending in another currency; Ramp, card spending in another currency

Can I tell whether I qualify, and what happens after I start?

Few first screens said who can apply or what happens next

I followed each platform's main start button to its first screen in a browser. Loop's sign-up was the only one that listed the steps ahead and what they need (CRA ID, date of birth, government ID). Ramp states its eligibility rules (entity, Business Number, Canadian bank account) on its card page, but its sign-up screen asks only for an email. Venn's sign-up says there is no personal credit check, while its Quebec exclusion appears elsewhere on the site. Float's demo form and Airwallex's sign-up ask for contact details without saying what follows, and Keep's start buttons lead to a closed-applications page. Denominator: 6 of 6 first screens observed in a browser on 23 October 2026; none was followed past the first screen.

Evidence: Loop, application or demo journey; Ramp, eligibility; Ramp, application or demo journey; Venn, application or demo journey; Venn, availability; Float, application or demo journey; Airwallex, application or demo journey; Keep, availability

What this means for your marketing team

When a headline offer and its condition live on different pages, or a form says nothing about what comes next, the reader has to fill the gap. Ask your team to list every place your main offer appears and check that its condition travels with it, and to read your first form as a stranger would.

How this research was done

Selection

The founder selected these six platforms as a comparison set of providers that market business cards and spend management to Canadian businesses. They are not presented as the largest, the most used or the best, and other providers exist.

Dates

Public pages were fetched by a server on 23 October 2026 from 10:39 a.m. to 10:48 a.m. Toronto time, a browser pass checked them from 12:00 p.m. to 12:14 p.m., and two more pages were fetched later the same day: the Airwallex fee schedule at 2:10 p.m. and the Float Payments page at 2:33 p.m. Every observation carries its own capture time.

Public sources only

I read only public pages: no accounts, logins, forms submitted, credit checks or transactions. A server fetch does not run JavaScript, so a browser pass then checked what visitors see, followed each enquiry journey to its first screen and looked up the FINTRAC and Bank of Canada registries. Where the two differed, the page reports what a visitor sees and keeps the server reading in the evidence.

Limitations

  • Pages change. Every observation is dated to its capture and may no longer be current.
  • The browser pass used a normal Chrome profile that was not signed in to any of the six providers. Journeys were followed only to the first screen; nothing was submitted.
  • US and global pages were not used as Canadian facts. Where a Canadian page carried US disclosures, that is recorded as an observation about the page.
  • Regulatory statements and registry entries (FINTRAC and the Bank of Canada, checked on 23 October 2026) are recorded in each platform's evidence as supporting detail. They are not a compliance assessment, an endorsement or a safety rating.
  • This edition does not compare FX costs or product performance and does not recommend a provider.

How each provider presents its offer: who it speaks to, what it promises, where the conditions sit and what proof it gives.

Selected communication examples

Four observed examples of how an offer, proof or next step was explained. They are examples to learn from, not winning platforms.

Proof · Loop

A savings figure labelled as the provider's own estimate

The Bells of Steel case study presents $10,000 a year in savings and says in the text that Loop calculated it. The same site's homepage averages ($40,000 and 12 hours a year) carry no basis I could find, so the labelled figure stands out.

My reading

A reader can weigh a number when they know who calculated it and how.

Evidence: Loop, case study; Loop, proof

Offer explanation · Float

"Up to" with the threshold in a numbered footnote

Float's pages say "Up to 1% cashback". The pricing page footnote gives the condition: 1% applies above $25k of monthly spending per card program. The rate below that threshold was not on the pages I reviewed.

My reading

"Up to" signals a condition; the footnote states it, but only on the pricing page.

Evidence: Float, rewards

Positioning · Airwallex

A competitor named inside a customer story

Airwallex's Adaria Vending Services story, about a Canadian company expanding into the US, names Venn and Wise as tools the company used before. The outcomes it reports are qualitative, such as cards issued in under 24 hours.

My reading

Naming the prior setup makes the switch concrete; the outcome is still the provider's account of its customer.

Evidence: Airwallex, case study

Venn

What the sources say

Who it is for and the main promise
Canadian business banking with accounts, cards and transfers in one place, for businesses outside Quebec.
Offer and its conditions
1% cashback, no FX fee from matching-currency balances, and plans from $0. The cards page says the cashback has no caps; the pricing footnote says it is earned only on the first $5,000 of card spend a month on Essentials and the first $25,000 on Plus.
Funding, rewards and FX explained
The funding model is clear in the agreement (funds must be in the account first). The FX fee by plan is on pricing and in the agreement, separate from the no-FX-fee line on the cards page. The number of currencies you can send is given as 36 on two pages and 33 in a help article.
Published proof
The visible homepage counts agree at 15,000+ (a 4,000+ figure is in the page code but set not to display). The Flow case study reports time saved without figures. The transfers page claims FX rates up to 2.75% cheaper than banks, with no basis I could find.
My reading

The cashback statement on the cards page and the footnote on pricing cannot both be read literally for Essentials and Plus customers.

Missing evidence. Steps after account creation (needs an account).

What this means for your marketing team

Your cards page and your pricing page are read as one offer. Ask your team whether a visitor who sees only your highest-traffic page would know which spend earns the reward on each plan.

What this means for your marketing team

Buyers of financial products look for the basis behind a number and for the plan that includes the feature they need. Ask your team which figure on your site a careful buyer would question first, and whether each feature page says which plan includes it.

Three tests for a marketing team

Three tests a marketing team could run on its own pages, each tied to a finding above. They are proposals to discuss, not results, and none needs a provider's internal data.

  1. Proposed test, not a resultPut the reward condition beside the rate (from finding 1)
    Observed example
    Venn's cards page says its cashback has no caps while its pricing footnote limits the eligible card spend on two plans; Loop's cards page says rewards on all spend while its earn-rate table lists USD, EUR and GBP spend as not eligible on Basic.
    Customer uncertainty
    Will I earn the advertised rate on my plan and at my spend level?
    Change, and what stays the same
    Show the cap, threshold or plan condition in the same block as the headline rate on the card page. Keep the rate, the design, the button and the terms the same.
    Primary measure
    Qualified applications or demo requests (meeting your eligibility rules) per visitor to the card page.
    Quality guardrail
    Rewards questions to sales and support in the first 30 days after sign-up, and plan mix.
    Comparison method
    A randomised split of card-page traffic with the same sources, the same attribution window and the same definition of a qualified application. Count button clicks separately from qualified applications.
    When the result would be inconclusive
    If rewards are not a main reason buyers choose you, or traffic is too small to separate the two versions, the result will be inconclusive. Legal review of the wording comes first.
  2. Proposed test, not a resultSay which transactions a "no FX fee" claim covers (from finding 2)
    Observed example
    All six platforms' no-fee statements covered specific cases (a matching balance, four currencies, a USD card), with other conversions priced elsewhere.
    Customer uncertainty
    Will I pay to convert when I buy in a currency I do not hold?
    Change, and what stays the same
    Replace a general "no FX fees" line with one that names the case, followed by one line saying what applies otherwise. Keep the page layout, offer and button the same.
    Primary measure
    Completed applications from the FX or cards page.
    Quality guardrail
    FX-related support questions and disputed charges in the first 60 days after sign-up.
    Comparison method
    A randomised split with the same audiences and attribution window; if traffic is low, run the versions in matched consecutive periods and record the dates.
    When the result would be inconclusive
    Required disclosures stay in place; this changes the summary, not the terms. Seasonal travel or trade cycles can swamp the difference.
  3. Proposed test, not a resultState eligibility and the next step before the form (from finding 3)
    Observed example
    Loop's sign-up lists the two steps ahead and the documents they need; Ramp's eligibility rules sit on its card page rather than its sign-up screen; the other first screens asked for contact or account details without saying what follows.
    Customer uncertainty
    Do I qualify, and what happens after I give my email?
    Change, and what stays the same
    Add a short "Before you start" list above the first form field: who can apply, what you need, which provinces are served, and what happens after submitting. Keep the form fields and verification steps the same.
    Primary measure
    The share of form starts that become qualified applications.
    Quality guardrail
    Total form starts and completed submissions from eligible businesses, so the list does not turn away people who qualify.
    Comparison method
    A randomised split with consistent audiences; report clicks, form starts, submissions and qualified applications as separate numbers.
    When the result would be inconclusive
    If eligibility rules change during the test, or most traffic is already qualified, the result will not separate the two versions. Never remove necessary identity or eligibility checks.

There are no baselines, uplift figures or sample sizes here on purpose: they depend on your own traffic and data.
